\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

This project independently implements paper-aligned FCI and FCI+, validates
them against exact graphical oracles and established software, and separates
algorithm fidelity from a robust finite-sample application workflow.

The scientific difficulty is that latent common causes and selection can make
local adjacency conditioning insufficient, while standard FCI's
Possible-D-SEP search can be expensive and sample conditional-independence
errors can propagate into PAG endpoints.  The implementation therefore
provides both the historical search schedule of \citet{spirtes2000causation}
and Algorithm~2 of \citet{claassen2013learning}, whose
\(O(N^{2(k+2)})\) result is a conditional-independence query bound under a
fixed true observed-MAG degree bound \(k\), faithfulness, and an exact
constant-time oracle.

Validation proceeds in layers: unit and property tests; exact MAG
m-separation fixtures; the published Figure~4(b) MAG and exhibited separator
\(\{U,V,Z\}\); a 15-run, five-scenario known-truth benchmark; executed
differential comparisons with causal-learn~0.1.4.7 and
\code{pcalg}~2.7-12; and finite-sample order, bootstrap, sensitivity, and
chronology audits.  The endpoint-complete oracle targets are independently
specified repository fixtures rather than all being PAGs printed in the
sources.

On the committed known-truth benchmark, robust FCI+ has mean skeleton F1
0.985, semantic-edge F1 0.985, and endpoint accuracy 0.840, compared with
0.977, 0.943, and 0.737 for paper-profile FCI; its mean logical CI count is
402 rather than 450.  Paper-profile FCI+ and the two executed external
references have mean semantic-edge F1 0.951 under the recorded settings.
These results describe the tested cases and policies; they do not establish
general statistical dominance.

The separate Tennessee STAR application analyses 6,325 kindergarten-cohort
students from the official 11,601-record dataset.  Its strongest causal
evidence is the randomized-arm contrast: small-class assignment is associated
with 13.9 points higher observed kindergarten achievement than regular-class
assignment (school-cluster 95\% interval 5.2 to 22.1), whereas the
regular-with-aide contrast is near zero.  Later grade-3 contrasts are
complete-case results and weaker evidence.  Across discovery methods,
skeleton agreement is more defensible than several order-, threshold-, or
chronology-sensitive endpoint marks; no PAG in this report identifies a
unique DAG or estimates a treatment effect.

\chapter*{Executive Summary}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Executive Summary}
\small

\textbf{Contribution.} This project independently implements paper-aligned
FCI and FCI+, validates them against exact graphical oracles and established
software, and separates algorithm fidelity from a robust finite-sample
application workflow.  The reusable package and the STAR analysis remain
separate layers: the former contains domain-neutral algorithms, while the
latter supplies design, coding, selection, and interpretation decisions.

\textbf{Source fidelity.} Standard FCI is mapped to the 2000 book's initial
adjacency search, collider phase, two ordered Possible-D-SEP searches, reset,
and historical orientation closure.  Its theorem establishes a correct
partially oriented inducing-path representation under correct CI information,
but explicitly did not establish maximal informativeness.  Complete modern
PAG orientation is therefore attributed separately to
\citet{zhang2008completeness}.  FCI+ is mapped to the augmented skeleton,
candidate D-sep-link recognition, recursive hierarchy, minimal separator, and
candidate-revisit stages of \citet[Algorithm~2, p.~178]
{claassen2013learning}.

\textbf{Validation outcome.} Exact-oracle tests establish recovery of the
independently specified targets on the encoded fixtures.  For the published
Figure~4(b) MAG, the source-verifiable claim is narrower: the false
\(X-Y\) link is removed with the exhibited separator \(\{U,V,Z\}\) and
FCI+ D-SEP provenance.  On that run, FCI+ uses 63 logical CI queries compared
with 102 for FCI.  The endpoint-complete target is repository derived, and
passing fixtures are not a proof for every faithful MAG.

\textbf{Established-software outcome.} A matched 15-run benchmark executes
local paper FCI, local paper FCI+, local robust FCI+,
causal-learn~0.1.4.7 FCI, and \code{pcalg}~2.7-12
\code{fciPlus}.  Robust FCI+ improves the mean endpoint-oriented metrics on
this benchmark at a higher query cost than paper FCI+, while paper FCI+,
causal-learn, and \code{pcalg} agree in their aggregate semantic metrics under
the recorded settings.  Tetrad~7.6.10 is a documentation-only comparison
because it was not executed.  The local improvement claim is integrated
auditability---paper-profile control, CI/orientation traces, separator-source
provenance, D-SEP diagnostics, per-edge explanations, stability audits, and
bundled artifacts---not universal superiority.

\textbf{Independent implementation outcome.} CRAN \code{pcalg} 2.7-12 under
R 4.5.3 was run on the same encoded panels with a Python-equivalent G-square
test. It is treated as a differential reference, not ground truth. The focused
PAG is exactly identical across all three algorithms. In the attrition and
longitudinal panels, pairwise skeleton Jaccard values range from 0.765 to
0.929, but exact endpoint agreement on shared edges is lower. Cyclic variable
orders preserve every \code{pcalg} skeleton yet reproduce the original PAG
endpoints in only 67--78\% of orders.

\textbf{Robust application outcome.} The paper implementations remain
unchanged for fidelity testing. A separate practical profile is used for
empirical interpretation. Across all 26 cyclic column shifts it reproduces
each panel's complete PAG exactly, retains the main target adjacencies in every
order, and replaces chronology-conflicting definite arrows with unresolved
circle endpoints. This improves stability without claiming new causal
identification.

\textbf{STAR outcome.} The official data were converted into three independent
application panels:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
  \item an attrition panel containing 5,744 students and a grade-3 observation
        indicator;
  \item a longitudinal panel containing 2,787 complete cases and both
        kindergarten and grade-3 achievement;
  \item a focused complete-case panel containing 2,976 students, excluding
        kindergarten achievement from the discovery node set.
\end{enumerate}

The strongest result does not come from a PAG endpoint. It comes from the
randomized-arm comparison: small classes have higher observed kindergarten
scores than regular classes, whereas adding an aide to a regular class does
not show a comparable advantage. The later grade-3 contrast is also positive
among observed students, but attrition prevents the same strength of causal
interpretation.

\textbf{Causal interpretation.}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
  \item The randomized design, together with the positive school-cluster
        interval, supports a beneficial causal effect of small classes on
        early achievement for students with observed kindergarten outcomes.
  \item There is no corresponding evidence that a regular class with an aide
        improves achievement relative to a regular class.
  \item Kindergarten achievement is the most stable structural correlate of
        grade-3 achievement and grade-3 observation.
  \item The focused bidirected treatment-outcome edge is replicated exactly by
        all three algorithms, but is threshold-sensitive and cannot override
        knowledge of randomization.
  \item Cross-implementation skeleton consensus is more credible than endpoint
        marks that conflict with chronology or change under variable order.
  \item The robust application profile supports the same main adjacencies but
        leaves their directions unresolved; this is the preferred
        finite-sample reading of the discovery output.
  \item PAG endpoints should be read as equivalence-class constraints, not
        regression coefficients or direct effects.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Bottom line.} The case study demonstrates practical use of the
algorithm and reveals where selection and modelling choices matter. It does
not convert exploratory causal discovery into a substitute for design-based
effect estimation.

\part{Algorithm, Implementation, and Validation}

\chapter{Introduction}

\section{Motivation}

Many empirical studies ask causal questions in settings where not every common
cause has been measured. Family background, institutional selection, health,
motivation, and administrative recording processes may all generate
dependence that cannot be represented honestly by a causally sufficient DAG
over the recorded columns. Fitting a directed graph while silently assuming
causal sufficiency can turn uncertainty into unjustified arrows.

FCI was designed for this problem. Under causal Markov, faithfulness, and
appropriate sampling assumptions, it uses conditional-independence
information to learn a PAG that remains valid when latent confounders and
selection variables may exist \citep{spirtes2000causation,
richardson2002ancestral}. The price of this broader model class is additional
search and greater endpoint ambiguity. In particular, standard FCI's
Possible-D-SEP refinement may test many separating sets that are not contained
in local adjacency neighbourhoods.

\FCIplus{} addresses the search problem for sparse causal graphs.
\citet{claassen2013learning} provide a logical characterisation of D-SEP links
and a hierarchical procedure for finding the relevant separating sets. For a
fixed maximum degree \(k\), their result yields a polynomial worst-case bound
in the number \(N\) of observed variables, commonly written
\[
  O\!\left(N^{2(k+2)}\right).
\]
This is a sparsity-dependent guarantee, not a statement that every finite
dataset will be easy or statistically well behaved.

\section{Project contributions}

The central contribution is stated as one auditable proposition:

\keyresult{This project independently implements paper-aligned FCI and FCI+,
validates them against exact graphical oracles and established software, and
separates algorithm fidelity from a robust finite-sample application
workflow.}

That proposition contains two distinct layers.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
  \item \textbf{Algorithm contribution.} A self-contained Python implementation
        of FCI and \FCIplus{}, with multiple CI tests, explicit PAG endpoint
        operations, paper-aligned profiles, practical finite-sample profiles,
        diagnostics, stability selection, type annotations, and release
        validation.
  \item \textbf{Application contribution.} A separate Tennessee STAR case
        study that applies the public algorithm API without importing
        STAR-specific assumptions into the package. It uses randomized-arm
        contrasts as an external design-based reference and PAG learning as a
        structural sensitivity analysis.
\end{enumerate}

This separation is central to the research design. An algorithm should remain
reusable and domain-neutral; an application must state its own coding,
selection, measurement, clustering, and interpretation decisions.

\section{Research questions}

The report addresses five questions:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em,label=\textbf{RQ\arabic*.}]

  \item Does the implementation map those stages to explicit code, public

  \item What is recovered under exact-oracle and finite-sample known-truth

\end{enumerate}

\chapter{Causal-Graphical Background}

\section{Conditional independence and graphical assumptions}

Let \(V\) be the set of observed variables, \(L\) latent variables, and \(S\)
selection variables. Constraint-based discovery begins from statements of the
form
\[
  X \perp Y \mid Z,
\]
where \(Z\subseteq V\setminus\{X,Y\}\). A causal graph and probability
distribution are linked by two central assumptions.

\textbf{Causal Markov condition.} Graphical separation implies probabilistic
conditional independence.

\textbf{Faithfulness.} Probabilistic conditional independences arise from
graphical separation rather than exact cancellation of causal effects.

Under causal sufficiency these assumptions support DAG-based methods such as
PC. When latent or selection variables are present, the appropriate
observed-variable object is an ancestral graph or MAG
\citep{richardson2002ancestral}. Different MAGs may encode the same
conditional-independence model, so the identifiable output is a PAG rather
than one unique graph.

\subsection{DAG, latent projection, MAG, and PAG targets}

A DAG is a causal representation over a specified variable set.  Marginalizing
latent variables and conditioning on selection variables can induce directed,
bidirected, and undirected relations among the observed nodes.  An ancestral
graph represents those relations without explicitly drawing the hidden or
selection nodes; a maximal ancestral graph (MAG) makes every missing edge
correspond to an m-separation.  A PAG then records exactly the endpoint
features shared by the MAGs in one observational Markov equivalence class
\citep{richardson2002ancestral,zhang2008completeness}.

M-separation generalizes d-separation to mixed ancestral graphs.  It is the
criterion used by the exact graphical oracle in this project.  A sample
Fisher-Z or G-square test, by contrast, is an error-prone statistical decision
about a distribution.  Oracle recovery evidence therefore isolates graph
logic; it cannot be transferred directly to finite-sample accuracy.

\subsection{Adjacency, ancestry, and intervention are different claims}

An adjacency says that the measured variables were not separated by the
tested sets under the recorded policy.  A tail or arrowhead can constrain
ancestry across the represented equivalence class.  Neither fact alone
identifies a direct structural coefficient, an adjustment set, or an
interventional contrast.  Selection variables can also induce undirected
marks.  These distinctions determine the evidence hierarchy used in the STAR
application.

\chapter{Standard FCI in Spirtes et al. (2000)}
\label{chap:standard-fci-source}

\section{Historical target and assumptions}

The Fast Causal Inference algorithm was developed for a problem that is
strictly broader than causal discovery under causal sufficiency.  Let \(O\)
denote the measured variables and let the full acyclic causal graph also
contain variables that are not in \(O\).  FCI reasons from conditional
independences in the marginal distribution over \(O\); it does not require
that all common causes of measured variables are themselves measured
\citep[Chapter~6, \S\S6.3 and 6.7, pp.~129--130 and 139]
{spirtes2000causation}.

The population argument rests on two assumptions about the full causal system.
The Causal Markov condition links graphical separation to probabilistic
independence, while Faithfulness excludes additional independences created by
special parameter cancellations
\citep[Chapter~3, \S\S3.4.1 and 3.4.3, pp.~29--31]
{spirtes2000causation}.  The algorithmic theorem additionally presumes that
the conditional-independence decisions supplied to the search are correct.
These premises matter operationally.  A sample Fisher-Z or G-square decision
is an estimate of the oracle answer; it is not the oracle assumed by the
theorem.

The terminology also has a historical boundary.  The second edition states
its output as a \emph{partially oriented inducing path graph}.  The modern
ancestral-graph and MAG/PAG formulation was developed subsequently
\citep{richardson2002ancestral}.  This report uses PAG notation because it is
the natural representation in the implementation, but it does not retroactively
attribute every modern PAG result to the 2000 book.  In particular, complete
orientation of all invariant marks depends on the later rule set of
\citet{zhang2008completeness}.

\cautionbox{In this chapter, ``book-aligned FCI'' means the search and
orientation schedule in \citet[Chapter~6, especially pp.~139--145]
{spirtes2000causation}.  ``Completed PAG'' refers to the later MAG/PAG
formalism and completeness results.  The two descriptions are related, but
they are not historically interchangeable.}

\section{Why adjacency-only conditioning is insufficient}

A PC-style adjacency search removes \(X-Y\) when it finds a set \(S\) among
the current local neighbours such that \(X\perp Y\mid S\).  Under causal
sufficiency, this local strategy has a strong justification.  With latent
variables, however, a pair may remain adjacent after local search even though
it is separable over the observed variables.  A necessary separator can
contain a node that is no longer adjacent to either endpoint by the time the
search reaches the relevant conditioning depth.

The book introduces D-SEP to characterize where an observed separator can
reside in the underlying inducing-path structure
\citep[Chapter~6, \S6.7, p.~141]{spirtes2000causation}.  D-SEP itself is not
directly available from the partially learned graph.  FCI therefore constructs
the observable superset Possible-D-SEP and performs a second, non-local
separation search.  This second search is the central difference between
standard FCI and a purely adjacency-based constraint procedure.

The cost is deliberate.  Enlarging the candidate set protects against a false
retained adjacency, but it can require many high-order CI queries.  Thus FCI's
latent-variable protection and its computational burden arise from the same
mechanism.

The corresponding control flow can be expressed without importing later

\begin{lstlisting}[style=python,
caption={Source-faithful control-flow summary of standard FCI.}]
def fci_2000(observed_variables, independence_oracle):
    graph = complete_circle_graph(observed_variables)
    graph, sepsets = pc_adjacency_search(graph, independence_oracle)

    orient_unshielded_colliders(graph, sepsets)
    search_graph = graph.copy()

    for x, y in search_graph.edges():
        pools = (
            possible_dsep(search_graph, x, y),
            possible_dsep(search_graph, y, x),
        )
        for pool in pools:                 # ordered directions stay separate
            separator = first_subset_separating(x, y, pool)
            if separator is not None:
                graph.remove_edge(x, y)
                sepsets[x, y] = separator
                break

    reset_endpoint_marks(graph)
    orient_unshielded_colliders(graph, sepsets)
    apply_original_orientation_closure(graph, sepsets)
    return graph
\end{lstlisting}

\section{Possible-D-SEP}

For an ordered pair \((A,B)\), a node \(V\ne A\) belongs to
\(\operatorname{Possible\text{-}D\text{-}SEP}(A,B)\) when there is a path from
\(A\) to \(V\) such that every internal triple on the path satisfies at least
one of two conditions:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
  \item the middle node is a collider on that path; or
  \item the middle node is not a definite noncollider and the two outer nodes
        are adjacent, so the triple forms a triangle.
\end{enumerate}

This is the definition immediately preceding the Fast Causal Inference
Algorithm in \citet[Chapter~6, \S6.7, p.~144]{spirtes2000causation}.  The
triangle clause is not a claim that the middle node is a true collider.  It
retains a node when the current partial marks do not yet justify excluding
it.  Possible-D-SEP is therefore intentionally permissive: it is a
search-safe superset, not an estimate of a unique causal neighbourhood.

The asymmetry is also substantive.  The source requires candidate search from
the \(A\)-side and from the \(B\)-side.  A valid separator may be available
from one ordered construction but not the other.  An implementation that uses
only one direction, or that creates conditioning sets by mixing arbitrary
members of the two pools, is no longer executing the same search contract.

In the repository, \path{discovery/pdsep.py::possible_dsep} traverses ordered
edge states and applies the collider-or-triangle predicate in
\path{_is_pds_step_allowed}.  The traversal may conservatively include a node
reachable by a qualifying walk even when a simple-path implementation would
encounter it differently.  Such over-inclusion spends additional CI queries
but preserves the intended superset role.  The corresponding regression suite
checks endpoint exclusion, blocked paths, cycles, path limits, definite
noncollider tails, both ordered directions, and the rule that the two pools
must not be mixed.

\section{Collider and discriminating-path information}

For an unshielded triple \(A-B-C\), separator membership distinguishes the
collider and noncollider cases.  If
\[
  B\notin\operatorname{Sepset}(A,C),
\]
the book's Causal Inference Algorithm orients arrowheads into \(B\)
\citep[step C, p.~139]{spirtes2000causation}.  FCI applies this decision once
before Possible-D-SEP construction and again after the final skeleton is
known.  The second application is necessary because the non-local search can
change both the skeleton and the stored separator evidence.

The later orientation phase also uses definite discriminating paths.  For a
discriminated triple, inclusion of the middle node in the relevant separator
supports a noncollider conclusion, while exclusion supports a collider
conclusion
\citep[definition and Figure~6.14, pp.~138--140]
{spirtes2000causation}.  The source repeats the orientation clauses to closure.
The modern labels R1--R10 should not be projected wholesale onto this passage:
the original book supplies the historical arrowhead and discriminating-path
phase, whereas later work supplies a complete PAG rule schedule.

\section{Theorem scope and the historical completeness boundary}

Theorem~6.4 states that, under Faithfulness and correct population-level
independence decisions, FCI returns a partially oriented inducing path graph
for the observed margin
\citep[p.~145]{spirtes2000causation}.  This is a correctness result for the
historical target.  It supports the following bounded statement:

\keyresult{Under the book's graphical assumptions and correct CI decisions,
the original FCI schedule returns a correct partially oriented inducing-path
representation for the observed variables.}

The sentence immediately following the theorem is equally important: the
authors state that they did not know whether the output was always maximally
informative.  Therefore Theorem~6.4 does \emph{not} prove orientation
completeness for a modern completed PAG.  The repository preserves this
distinction with two orientation strategies:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
  \item \code{orientation\_strategy="spirtes\_2000"} stops after the
        book-aligned historical closure; and
  \item \code{orientation\_strategy="standard"} additionally applies the later
        Zhang-style tail-completion rules required for a complete modern PAG
        \citep{zhang2008completeness}.
\end{itemize}

The distinction is not cosmetic.  A graph with more circles can be correct
but less informative.  Conversely, describing a later, more extensively
oriented graph as the literal output theorem of the 2000 source overstates
what that source established.

\section{What is not established by the 2000 source}
\label{sec:fci-not-established}

The following propositions are outside the theorem and algorithm statements
in \citet{spirtes2000causation}:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
  \item \textbf{No finite-sample recovery guarantee.}  The source guarantee is
        conditional on correct CI decisions.  It does not prove that Fisher-Z,
        G-square, kernel CI, or a missing-data test will recover the oracle
        decisions at a given \(N\).
  \item \textbf{No universal tractability claim.}  Possible-D-SEP may be large,
        and its subset search can remain expensive.  The book discusses
        favourable sparse structures; it does not give the fixed-degree
        polynomial query bound later proved for \FCIplus{}.
  \item \textbf{No 2000 proof of maximal orientation.}  Maximal
        informativeness is explicitly left open after Theorem~6.4.
  \item \textbf{No unique DAG.}  The output represents observationally
        indistinguishable causal structures under the assumptions.
  \item \textbf{No direct-effect or treatment-effect estimate.}  A retained
        adjacency is not automatically a direct structural coefficient, and
        the algorithm does not estimate an intervention contrast.
  \item \textbf{No identified latent variable from one edge mark.}  A
        bidirected endpoint pattern is compatible with relevant ancestry
        restrictions, but it does not name, count, or measure a hidden cause.
  \item \textbf{No guarantee under truncated search.}  Finite conditioning or
        path limits, or disabling Possible-D-SEP, may leave a false adjacency
        and are not covered by the unbounded source schedule.
\end{enumerate}


\chapter{FCI+ in Claassen et al. (2013)}
\label{chap:fci-plus-source}

\section{Problem statement and restricted complexity result}

\citet{claassen2013learning} ask whether causal structure discovery remains
intrinsically exponential when the observed causal structure is sparse but
latent variables and selection bias may be present.  Their formal
\code{C-LEARN} task assumes:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
  \item an independence oracle that answers any
        \(X\perp Y\mid Z\) query exactly and in constant time;
  \item a distribution faithful to an underlying causal DAG;
  \item an observed-variable MAG whose maximum node degree is bounded by a
        constant \(k\); and
  \item a completed PAG as the output target.
\end{enumerate}

These assumptions appear in Definition~1 and the \code{C-LEARN} statement on
p.~173, the input to Algorithm~2 on p.~178, and the complexity analysis on
p.~179 of the UAI paper.  Under them, the authors establish the worst-case
conditional-independence query bound
\[
  O\!\left(N^{2(k+2)}\right),
\]
where \(N\) is the number of observed variables and \(k\) is treated as a
constant.

The claim is deliberately narrower than the paper's memorable title.  It is a
polynomial query-complexity result for a sparse, faithful oracle problem.
It neither solves an arbitrary NP-hard structure-learning problem nor makes a
finite-sample statistical test exact or constant-time.  The paper itself
states that the result does not contradict known hardness results
\citep[abstract and introduction, pp.~172--173]{claassen2013learning}.

\section{D-separated pairs, D-sep nodes, and D-sep links}

After a bounded PC adjacency search, some nonadjacent pairs in the true MAG
can remain falsely connected because every useful separator requires a node
outside the current endpoint-adjacency union.  Definition~2 on p.~176 names
such a pair D-separated and calls the surviving false adjacency a D-sep link
\citep{claassen2013learning}.  A required nonadjacent member of the separator
is a D-sep node.

The capitalization matters.  D-SEP is the authors' named search construct in
a MAG setting.  The paper's surrounding preliminaries use m-separation for
mixed ancestral graphs even though Definition~2 uses the phrase
``d-separated.''  This report therefore uses \emph{D-sep link} for the named
algorithmic object and \emph{m-separation} for the exact graphical oracle.

Figure~4(b), pp.~175--176, gives the canonical five-node example.  Its MAG has
the six edges
\[
  Z\to U,\quad Z\to V,\quad
  U\leftrightarrow X,\quad V\to X,\quad
  U\to Y,\quad V\leftrightarrow Y.
\]
The initial adjacency search retains a false \(X-Y\) link, while
\[
  X\perp Y\mid\{U,V,Z\}
\]
and \(Z\) is not adjacent to either endpoint.  The source exhibits this
separator and identifies \(Z\) as the D-sep node.  It does not print the
repository's completed-PAG endpoint target, and it does not state that the
exhibited separator is uniquely minimal.

\section{The temporary augmented skeleton}

FCI+ does not search every subset of standard FCI's potentially large
Possible-D-SEP pool.  It first extracts additional invariant non-ancestry
information into an \emph{augmented skeleton} \(G^+\).  The construction uses
a single-node minimal-dependence test.  If a known minimal independence
\[
  X\perp Y\mid S
\]
becomes a dependence when a suitable adjacent node \(A\) is added,
\[
  X\not\!\perp Y\mid S\cup\{A\},
\]
Lemma~2(1) supports an invariant arrowhead at \(A\) on the relevant incident
edges.  Definition~3 and the following paragraph on p.~176 define the
augmentation; Algorithm~2 rebuilds it at lines 2 and 16
\citep{claassen2013learning}.

The arrowheads in \(G^+\) are search marks.  They help recognize candidate
D-sep links; they are not automatically final PAG orientations.  The
repository mirrors this distinction by copying the current skeleton in
\path{build_augmented_skeleton}, using the copy during the hierarchical
search, and resetting the surviving output skeleton before final collider and
PAG orientation.

\section{Recognizing candidate D-sep links}

Lemma~4 on p.~176 supplies the characteristic augmented-skeleton pattern.
Subject to the hierarchy condition developed in the paper, a D-sep link
\(X-Y\) appears as the middle edge of
\[
  U\leftrightarrow X\leftrightarrow Y\leftrightarrow V,
\]
where \(U\) and \(V\) are nonadjacent and the required cross-direction paths
do not run against an arrowhead.  The Appendix~A proof sketch on p.~180
explains why the local marks expose the otherwise non-local separator
\citep{claassen2013learning}.

This pattern only proposes a candidate.  It is not itself a CI decision.
FCI+ must still build a hierarchy-derived conditioning set and query the
oracle.  The distinction protects the soundness of the removal stage:
arrowhead patterns identify where to search; independence evidence determines
whether the edge is deleted.

The implementation keeps the pattern literal.  The middle and witness edges
must be bidirected in the augmented graph; unresolved circle marks are not
accepted as substitutes for invariant arrowheads.
\path{_has_dsep_link_witness} also checks witness nonadjacency and the two
not-against-arrowhead reachability conditions.  The associated tests include
positive witnesses, missing cross paths, missing target arrowheads, and
circle-only negative cases.

\section{Recursive hierarchy and removal order}

Definitions~4--5 and Lemmas~6--7 on p.~177 define the hierarchy
\(\operatorname{HIE}(X,I)\).  Starting from a seed set, it repeatedly adds
every node that occurs in a recorded minimal separator between a pair of
nodes already in the hierarchy.  In fixed-point form,
\[
\begin{split}
  H_0 &= X,\\
  H_{t+1}
    &= H_t\ \cup\
       \bigcup_{\substack{A,B\in H_t\\A\ne B}}
       \operatorname{Sepset}_{I}(A,B),\\
  \operatorname{HIE}(X,I) &= H_t
       \quad\text{when }H_{t+1}=H_t.
\end{split}
\]
Here \(I\) is the paper's collection of minimal independence statements.
Lemma~6 explains why a required D-sep node appears in another relevant
minimal separator, while Lemma~7 shows how the hierarchy seeded by suitable
adjacent ancestors contains a separator for the target D-sep link
\citep[pp.~177--178]{claassen2013learning}.

Candidate D-sep links can depend on one another.  Lemma~5 gives an acyclic
ordering argument: removal of one ancestral D-sep link may reveal the
information required to remove another, but the dependencies need not be
solved simultaneously.  Section~4.3 and Algorithm~2 implement this reasoning
by rebuilding the augmented skeleton and reconsidering candidates after each
successful deletion.

The repository function \path{hierarchy} performs this fixed-point expansion
and explicitly excludes the target pair's own separator from recursive
lookup.  The D-SEP refinement caches expansions by both the seed set and the
excluded target pair.  That cache is an engineering optimization; the
hierarchy definition and candidate-revisit logic are the paper mechanisms.

A compact implementation-neutral transcription is:

\begin{lstlisting}[style=python,
caption={Control-flow summary of Claassen et al.'s Algorithm 2.}]
def fci_plus(observed_variables, independence_oracle, k):
    graph, independence_set = pc_adjacency_search(
        observed_variables, independence_oracle, depth=k
    )
    augmented = augment(graph, independence_set, independence_oracle)
    candidates = possible_dsep_links(augmented)

    while candidates:
        x, y = candidates.pop()
        for zx in endpoint_bases(augmented, x, exclude=y, max_size=k):
            for zy in endpoint_bases(augmented, y, exclude=x, max_size=k):
                seed = {x, y, *zx, *zy}
                h = hierarchy(seed, independence_set, exclude_pair=(x, y))
                if independence_oracle.independent(x, y, h - {x, y}):
                    separator = minimize(h - {x, y})
                    graph.remove_edge(x, y)
                    independence_set[x, y] = separator
                    augmented = augment(
                        graph, independence_set, independence_oracle
                    )
                    candidates = possible_dsep_links(augmented)
                    break

    reset_endpoint_marks(graph)
    orient_colliders(graph, independence_set)
    apply_complete_pag_orientation(graph, independence_set)
    return graph
\end{lstlisting}

The listing emphasizes two properties sometimes lost in simplified
descriptions.  First, \(k\) bounds the endpoint bases because it is assumed to
bound the true observed-MAG degree; it is not merely a convenient runtime
knob.  Second, a successful edge removal changes the independence information
and therefore requires augmentation and candidate detection to be repeated.

\section{Query-complexity accounting}

The main paper's p.~179 accounting separates the stages:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
  \item the bounded PC adjacency search uses
        \(O(N^{k+2})\) CI queries;
  \item construction of the augmented skeleton uses \(O(N^3)\) queries;
  \item at most \(O(N^2)\) false-edge candidates are considered;
  \item the paired endpoint bases contribute \(O(N^{2k})\) choices for a
        candidate;
  \item separator minimization is separately bounded by \(O(N^2)\) queries,
        while repeated candidate reconsideration supplies an additional
        \(O(N^2)\) factor in the worst-case dominant accounting; and
  \item final graph orientation uses no additional CI queries, although it has
        its own graph-operation cost.
\end{itemize}

Consequently the dominant CI-query count is
\[
  O(N^2)\,O(N^{2k})\,O(N^2)
    = O\!\left(N^{2(k+2)}\right).
\]
This is the theorem/body value used throughout the report.  The arXiv proof
supplement's abstract states \(N^{2(k+1)}\), but its Theorem~1, proof, and
concluding sentence agree with the main UAI paper on \(N^{2(k+2)}\).  The
discrepancy is recorded rather than silently harmonized.

Logical query count must be distinguished from elapsed time.  The theorem
treats each oracle answer as a constant-time operation.  A sample CI test may
fit regressions, form contingency tables, perform matrix operations, or run
permutations; its cost can depend on sample size and conditioning-set
dimension.  Likewise, repository caching can reduce underlying test
executions without changing how often algorithmic control flow requests a
query.

\section{Orientation completeness is a dependency}

FCI+'s novel contribution is the sparse recovery of the correct skeleton and
separating information.  Algorithm~2 line 23 then invokes complete FCI
orientation.  The UAI paper does not rederive the full orientation-completeness
proof; it relies on established PAG orientation results and cites
\citet{zhang2008completeness}.  Therefore the complete-output claim requires
all of the following:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
  \item correct CI decisions and separators;
  \item correct identification and removal of every D-sep link;
  \item sound collider decisions; and
  \item a complete invariant-mark orientation schedule.
\end{enumerate}

In the repository, the paper profile selects
\code{orientation\_strategy="standard"}, which applies the later complete
schedule.  The historical \code{"spirtes\_2000"} schedule and the
\code{"conservative"}, \code{"leaf"}, and \code{"robust"} engineering
strategies intentionally have different orientation scopes and must not
inherit the complete-PAG theorem by label alone.

\section{What is not established by the 2013 source}
\label{sec:fciplus-not-established}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
  \item \textbf{No finite-sample superiority theorem.}  The paper does not
        prove that FCI+ is more accurate than standard FCI for a fixed sample,
        CI test, or significance threshold.
  \item \textbf{No empirical constant-time oracle.}  A statistical CI test is
        neither exact nor necessarily constant-time.
  \item \textbf{No polynomial guarantee for growing or wrong \(k\).}  The
        theorem assumes a constant bound on the true observed-MAG degree.  A
        user-entered \code{sparsity\_bound} is not evidence that the assumption
        is true.
  \item \textbf{No unrestricted complexity-class conclusion.}  The result is
        for the restricted sparse oracle problem, not every causal discovery
        formulation.
  \item \textbf{No wall-clock dominance.}  Query complexity does not rank
        programming languages, CI implementations, memory use, or practical
        runtimes.
  \item \textbf{No claim that every hierarchy is statistically efficient.}
        The discussion on p.~179 warns that hierarchy-derived conditioning
        sets may be large and may reduce finite-sample power.
  \item \textbf{No unique causal graph or effect estimate.}  The target is a
        completed PAG equivalence class, not a unique DAG or an intervention
        distribution.
  \item \textbf{No published completed PAG in Figure~4(b).}  The paper prints
        the MAG example and an exhibited separator.  A completed endpoint
        target must be derived and validated separately.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Public API and configuration}

The top-level functions keep standard FCI and FCI+ as separate entry points.
Paper profiles are explicit because a default optimized for finite samples
should not silently stand in for the source algorithm.

\code{FCIConfig.paper()} fixes the unbounded book-aligned search choices:

\code{FCIPlusConfig.paper(k=...)} requires an explicit \(k\), assigns that

The profiles constrain execution; they do not validate theorem assumptions.
Supplying \(k=3\) does not prove that the unknown true MAG has degree at most

reproduces the search schedule but does not create the constant-time exact
oracle assumed in the theory.

\section{PAG endpoint representation and mutation invariants}

The \code{PAG} class in \path{graph/pag.py} stores endpoints for ordered node
pairs while exposing each adjacency once.  Its endpoint accessor returns the
mark at \(y\) on edge \(x-y\).  This convention permits explicit
representation of tails, arrowheads, circles, and absence without flattening
a PAG into a directed graph.

Mutation operations validate both nodes, reject a one-sided or
\code{NONE}-marked edge, and require edge deletion through
\code{remove\_edge}.  Query methods distinguish adjacency,
possibly-directed ancestry, and directed-path ancestry.  These invariants
matter because an endpoint error can change both Possible-D-SEP reachability
and later orientation rules.

Tests in \path{tests/test_pag.py} exercise construction, node validation,
edge addition/removal, endpoint mutation, edge rendering, and copy
independence.  \path{tests/test_pag_queries.py} separately tests possible- and
definite-ancestor semantics.  The implementation therefore treats graph
storage as a tested algorithmic substrate, not a plotting-only convenience.

\section{Conditional-independence protocol, caching, and provenance}

Every search uses the abstract \code{CITest.test(data, x, y, cond\_set)}
contract and receives a structured \code{CITestResult}.  The built-in tests
cover Fisher-Z, missing-value Fisher-Z, chi-square, G-square, and kernel CI.
A custom test can implement the same protocol, which keeps search logic
independent of a particular data type.

\path{CITestCache} canonicalizes the unordered endpoint pair and conditioning
set.  Every request increments the logical query count and emits a
\code{CITraceEvent}; a repeated request also records that the result was a
cache hit.  Thus the result distinguishes:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
  \item the number of logical CI requests made by algorithmic control flow;
  \item the subset answered from the cache; and
  \item the underlying statistical test results, including method, statistic,
        \(p\)-value, effective sample size, and conditioning set.
\end{itemize}

This distinction is important when connecting measurements to the FCI+
theorem.  The theorem counts oracle queries.  Repository elapsed time includes
data preparation, graph operations, and the cost of uncached sample tests.
Neither count should be silently substituted for the other.

\section{Standard FCI pipeline}

\path{discovery.fci.FCI.fit} exposes the source stage ordering directly:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
  \item \path{_learn_initial_skeleton} creates a complete circle graph and
        calls \path{learn_initial_skeleton};
  \item \path{_orient_colliders} applies separator-based unshielded-collider
        decisions;
  \item \path{refine_skeleton_with_pdsep} searches subsets from the two ordered
        Possible-D-SEP pools and records removals with source
        \code{"pdsep"};
  \item \path{_orient_colliders(..., reset=True)} clears provisional endpoint
        marks and reconstructs collider information from the final sepsets;
        and
  \item \path{_apply_orientation_rules} runs the configured historical,
        complete, or conservative closure.
\end{enumerate}

The Possible-D-SEP traversal uses ordered edge states rather than enumerating
all complete simple paths.  Its step predicate accepts a collider or a
triangle whose center is not proven a noncollider by a tail.  The compact
representation does not separately encode the book's underlined-noncollider
notation.  It may conservatively over-include walk-reachable nodes; this is
compatible with the superset role but can increase CI work.  Dedicated tests
retain coverage of path blocking, cycles, tail-proven noncolliders, ordered
endpoint pools, and stable versus immediate update schedules.

\section{FCI+ pipeline}

\path{discovery.fci_plus.FCIPlus.fit} deliberately does not call standard
FCI's Possible-D-SEP refinement.  It passes the initial skeleton and
separating sets to
\path{refine_skeleton_with_fci_plus_dsep}, which implements the Algorithm~2
search:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
  \item \path{build_augmented_skeleton} copies the skeleton and adds

\section{What implementation evidence does not establish}
\label{sec:implementation-not-established}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
  \item \textbf{Tests are not a general proof.}  Passing unit tests and exact
        encoded fixtures establishes behavior on those cases, not correctness
        for every faithful MAG.
  \item \textbf{A hand-authored target is not an independent oracle.}  The
        Figure~4(b) MAG and exhibited separator are source based; the complete
        endpoint target is repository derived and requires independent
        equivalence-class validation before being called a published PAG.
  \item \textbf{A paper profile is not an assumption checker.}  It cannot
        verify faithfulness, causal acyclicity, exact CI information, or the
        true maximum MAG degree.
  \item \textbf{Robustness is benchmark bounded.}  Improved endpoint metrics
        or order stability on committed seeds and tested permutations do not
        imply universal finite-sample dominance.
  \item \textbf{Auditability is not causal validity.}  A complete trace can
        explain how a mark was produced while the underlying CI decision is
        still wrong.
  \item \textbf{External agreement is not ground truth.}  Agreement with
        \code{pcalg} or causal-learn is differential evidence about
        implementations, not proof of the causal graph.
  \item \textbf{A PAG is not a treatment-effect analysis.}  The output neither
        identifies one DAG nor estimates an adjustment set, intervention
        effect, mediation effect, or latent-variable value without additional
        assumptions and methods.
\end{enumerate}

These limitations motivate the report's separation between algorithm
validation and the Tennessee STAR application.  Exact graphical oracles test
paper-aligned logic under known independence information; finite-sample
benchmarks test empirical behavior under known simulated truth; established
software supplies differential evidence; and the STAR experiment supplies a
separate design-based basis for its strongest causal conclusion.

\section{Reference software}

The STAR analysis runs CRAN \code{pcalg} 2.7-12 under R 4.5.3 as an independent
reference implementation \citep{kalisch2012pcalg}. The runner calls
\code{pcalg::fciPlus} with selection-bias orientations enabled. It supplies a
custom G-square test that exactly mirrors the Python compact-table rule:
zero-marginal rows and columns are removed separately within each observed
conditioning stratum, and the resulting likelihood-ratio statistics and
degrees of freedom are summed. This avoids \code{disCItest}'s default
minimum-cell shortcut, which would otherwise change the CI decisions rather
than merely change the implementation.

The R output is treated as a differential reference, not ground truth. In
particular, \code{pcalg::fciPlus} does not expose the local implementation's
explicit \(k=3\) sparsity-bound parameter. Comparisons therefore report
skeleton overlap, exact endpoint agreement, target-edge marks, runtime, CI
calls, and cyclic-order sensitivity instead of awarding correctness by package
name.

\medskip
\keyresult{The oracle tests support the correctness of the implemented FCI and
\FCIplus{} search-and-orientation pipeline under exact conditional-independence
information. The finite-sample tests simultaneously show that empirical
results remain limited by CI-test error and tuning choices.}

\section{Fairness constraints and bounded improvement claim}

Every comparison obeys five rules.  First, circles, tails, and arrowheads are
preserved in semantic normalization.  Second, data, order, alpha, CI
semantics, and search caps are held fixed where interfaces allow and every
mismatch is disclosed.  Third, standard FCI, Claassen FCI+, and other
FCI-family hybrids are not relabelled as one algorithm.  Fourth, logical
queries, cache hits, executed statistical tests, and wall time remain
different quantities.  Fifth, smoke-call success establishes callability,
not scientific validity.

\keyresult{The local package integrates traceability and robustness tooling
that was not found as one public workflow in the checked interfaces. This is
an auditability and integration improvement, not a universal accuracy or
speed superiority claim.}

The robust profile's higher mean endpoint scores are therefore stated only
``on this benchmark.''  Its automatic alpha, stable deletion, max-\(p\)
separator choice, conservative collider policy, and cautious tail orientation
are engineering policies.  They can trade endpoint specificity and query cost
against tested stability; they are not revisions to the definition or theorem
of \citet{claassen2013learning}.

\part{Tennessee STAR Application}

\chapter{Study Design and Data}

\section{The Tennessee STAR experiment}

The Tennessee Student/Teacher Achievement Ratio project was a large
randomized experiment conducted in kindergarten through grade 3. Students and
teachers were assigned to small classes, regular classes, or regular classes
with a full-time aide. The official technical report describes small classes
of approximately 13--17 students and regular classes of approximately 22--25
students \citep{word1990star}. Subsequent economic analysis has used STAR as a
central source of evidence on class size and educational production
\citep{krueger1999experimental}.

The present analysis uses the official Harvard Dataverse release
\citep{achilles2008star}. The student-level file contains 11,601 records and
379 variables and is released under CC0 1.0. Data codes were checked against
the accompanying 146-page user guide.

\section{Why use causal discovery on a randomized experiment?}

Randomization already provides a stronger basis for estimating the class-size
effect than an unconstrained structure-learning algorithm. The purpose of FCI
is therefore not to rediscover or replace randomization. It is to examine:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
  \item whether grade-3 observation is structurally related to early
        achievement or background variables;
  \item whether early and later achievement remain adjacent after conditioning
        on the measured context;
  \item how the learned PAG changes when an early post-assignment outcome is
        included or excluded;
  \item whether FCI and \FCIplus{} agree on skeletons and important
        adjacencies;
  \item how much search work \FCIplus{} saves on a real dataset.
\end{itemize}

This design also provides an unusually useful external audit. If a data-only
PAG implies an implausible pre-treatment direction or suggests latent
confounding of a genuinely randomized assignment, the discrepancy should be
reported rather than accepted literally.

\chapter{STAR Analysis Methods}

\section{Descriptive randomized-arm reference}

Before causal discovery, the report computes observed means and
randomized-arm contrasts for:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
  \item the sum of kindergarten reading and mathematics scaled scores;
  \item the sum of grade-3 reading and mathematics scaled scores among
        students with both scores;
  \item the probability that both grade-3 scores are observed.
\end{enumerate}

Uncertainty is estimated with 1,000 bootstrap samples of kindergarten schools.
Resampling schools rather than individual students preserves the main
within-school dependence in the descriptive comparison. These contrasts are
reported separately from FCI output because they use the experiment's
assignment as the basis for interpretation.

\section{Discrete G-square conditional-independence test}

The application uses the likelihood-ratio G-square test. For variables \(X\)
and \(Y\), conditioning set \(S\), observed contingency counts \(O\), and
expected counts \(E\) under \(X\perp Y\mid S\), the statistic is
\[
  G^2 = 2\sum_{s}\sum_{x,y}
        O_{xys}\log\left(\frac{O_{xys}}{E_{xys}}\right),
\]
with zero-count contributions omitted. The test is appropriate for the
discretised panels, but high-order conditioning can create sparse cells. That
is one reason the \FCIplus{} bound is fixed at \(k=3\) and why sensitivity is
reported rather than hidden.

\section{School-cluster PAG stability}

For each panel and algorithm, the 79 kindergarten schools are sampled with
replacement. All student rows from a selected school are included, including
repeated copies when a school is sampled more than once. FCI or \FCIplus{} is
then refitted and the presence of each unordered adjacency is recorded.

With \(B=12\) replicates, the estimated adjacency frequency is
\[
  \widehat{\pi}_{XY}
  = \frac{1}{B}\sum_{b=1}^{B}
    \mathbf{1}\{X\text{ and }Y\text{ are adjacent in replicate }b\}.
\]
This is an exploratory stability diagnostic. Twelve replicates give a coarse
grid of 8.3 percentage points and are not sufficient for precise tail
probabilities.

\section{Sensitivity analysis}

\enlargethispage{3\baselineskip}

The focused treatment-outcome analysis is repeated for:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
  \item \(\alpha\in\{0.01,0.05\}\);
  \item three versus four quantile bins for age and achievement;
  \item standard FCI, paper-profile \FCIplus{}, and robust application
        \FCIplus{}.
\end{itemize}

The target is whether \code{K\_Class} remains adjacent to
\code{Grade3\_Achievement}. This directly tests whether the most
substantively tempting PAG edge is robust to reasonable analysis choices.

\section{Decision rule for robust conclusions}

The application distinguishes a reproducible adjacency from a credible
direction. A skeleton-level conclusion is promoted only when the target
adjacency survives every cyclic column shift and has high school-cluster
bootstrap frequency. A directional conclusion additionally requires invariant
endpoint marks, no conflict with measurement time or randomization, and no
material reversal under the alpha/binning sensitivity checks.

Failure of the directional rule does not delete the edge. It downgrades the
claim to conditional-dependence structure with unresolved direction. This
pre-specified rule is why the robust profile's circle endpoints are treated as
a reliability improvement rather than an incomplete result.

\section{Attrition and grade-3 observation}

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{pag_attrition.pdf}
\caption{Three PAGs for the attrition/observation panel: self-implemented FCI,
self-implemented \FCIplus{}, and R \code{pcalg::fciPlus}. The skeletons
substantially overlap, while the achievement/observation endpoint differs.}
\label{fig:pag-attrition}
\end{figure}

Standard FCI returns 14 edges after 1,705 CI tests; self-implemented
\FCIplus{} returns 17 edges after 793 tests; and R \code{pcalg::fciPlus}
returns 13 edges after 1,191 tests. The FCI/self-\FCIplus{},
FCI/R-\FCIplus{}, and self-/R-\FCIplus{} skeleton Jaccard values are 0.824,
0.929, and 0.765. All three omit a direct
\code{K\_Class}--\code{Grade3\_Observed} adjacency. In the self-implementation
school bootstrap that adjacency appears in only 17\% of FCI fits and 8\% of
both paper and robust \FCIplus{} fits.

By contrast, \code{K\_Achievement}--
\code{Grade3\_Observed} is adjacent in 100\% of the bootstrap fits for all
three local profiles. The corresponding free-lunch/observation adjacency is also stable
in the targeted audit. These results suggest that later data availability is
not independent of early achievement and measured socioeconomic or school
context.

The three endpoint readings are revealing. Standard FCI gives the impossible
\textit{grade-3 observation} \(\rightarrow\) \textit{kindergarten
achievement}; self-\FCIplus{} leaves a circle at the later endpoint; and
R-\FCIplus{} gives the temporally admissible
\textit{kindergarten achievement} \(\rightarrow\) \textit{grade-3
observation}. The R result is more plausible on this edge, but package identity
alone cannot establish that its orientation is statistically correct.

\cautionbox{The attrition PAG does not prove a particular missingness
mechanism. It shows that the observed conditional-independence structure links
grade-3 availability to earlier achievement and context. A formal
missing-data analysis would require an explicit estimand and additional
assumptions.}

\section{Longitudinal achievement}

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{pag_longitudinal.pdf}
\caption{Three PAGs for the longitudinal complete-case panel. All retain the
kindergarten/grade-3 achievement adjacency, but the two \FCIplus{}
implementations orient it against chronology.}
\label{fig:pag-longitudinal}
\end{figure}

Standard FCI returns 10 edges and 960 CI tests. Self-\FCIplus{} returns 13
edges and 335 tests, while R-\FCIplus{} returns 12 edges and 475 tests.
Pairwise skeleton Jaccard is 0.769 for FCI/self-\FCIplus{}, 0.833 for FCI/R,
and 0.923 for the two \FCIplus{} implementations.

All three algorithms retain the kindergarten/grade-3 achievement adjacency,
and it appears in all 12 school-cluster bootstrap samples for all three local
profiles.
Neither full-data PAG retains a direct class-assignment/grade-3 achievement
adjacency once kindergarten achievement is included. That treatment-outcome
adjacency nevertheless reappears in 50\% of FCI and 67\% of \FCIplus{}
paper-profile bootstrap fits. It appears in all robust-profile bootstrap fits
despite being absent from the robust full-data PAG. This discordance is a
warning that conservative orientation and order invariance do not remove
school-resampling sensitivity.

Both \FCIplus{} implementations orient grade-3 achievement toward
kindergarten achievement, which is temporally impossible if read causally.
Standard FCI leaves the grade-3 endpoint unresolved. The agreement between two
implementations is therefore not sufficient to override chronology: it may
replicate the same finite-sample CI pattern and orientation logic under
complete-case selection.

\section{Focused treatment-outcome panel}

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{pag_focused_treatment.pdf}
\caption{PAGs for the focused complete-case panel. All three algorithms recover
the exact same 10-edge skeleton and endpoint pattern, including the bidirected
class-assignment/grade-3 edge.}
\label{fig:pag-focused}
\end{figure}

Removing kindergarten achievement from the node set changes the result.
Standard FCI, self-\FCIplus{}, and R-\FCIplus{} all return the same 10-edge
PAG, not merely the same skeleton. All three contain
\[
  K\_\mathrm{Class}\leftrightarrow
  Grade3\_\mathrm{Achievement}.
\]
The adjacency appears in 92\% of the FCI school bootstraps, 83\% of the
paper self-\FCIplus{} bootstraps, and 100\% of the robust-profile bootstraps.
The independent exact PAG replication makes this
observed conditional-independence pattern more credible, but does not make the
literal latent-confounding interpretation credible.

A naive reading would call the bidirected edge evidence of latent confounding.
That reading is not defensible here. Kindergarten class assignment was
randomized, while the focused panel conditions on grade-3 outcomes being
observed and omits early post-assignment achievement. Conditioning on
observation, school-level dependence, omitted post-randomization variables,
and CI-test approximation can all create an observed independence pattern
that is represented by arrowheads at both endpoints.

\begin{figure}[H]

\section{Five tiers of evidence}

The application uses an explicit hierarchy so that a visually definite PAG
mark cannot outrank the experimental design.

\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\caption{Evidence hierarchy used for every STAR claim.}
\label{tab:evidence-hierarchy}
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{p{0.10\textwidth}
>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{0.28\textwidth}Y}
\toprule
Tier & Evidence & Permitted claim \\
\midrule
1 & Randomized assignment and school-cluster arm contrast &
Bounded causal statement for the analyzed observed outcome, qualified by
missingness, compliance, and unreconstructed assignment blocks. \\
2 & Adjacency replicated across methods, resamples, tested orders, and
sensitivity settings &
Robust skeleton-level structural association or constraint; no magnitude or
direction claim. \\
3 & Stable skeleton with circles or endpoint disagreement &
Adjacency is more credible than orientation; endpoint remains unidentified. \\
4 & Implementation-, order-, or chronology-sensitive endpoint &
Diagnostic evidence only; not a substantive direction. \\
5 & Threshold-sensitive or complete-case exploratory pattern &
Hypothesis for external validation, not a causal conclusion. \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{table}

FCI returns equivalence-class constraints and does not estimate a treatment
effect.  The randomized contrasts and the learned PAGs therefore answer
different questions even when they concern the same variables.

\section{What can be concluded}

\subsection{Small classes improve early observed achievement}

STAR's random assignment is the primary causal identification mechanism.
Within the kindergarten cohort and among students with observed score
components, the small-class arm is 13.9 points above the regular arm, with a
school-cluster interval that excludes zero. This supports a beneficial causal
effect of smaller classes on early achievement, subject to outcome
missingness, assignment compliance, and the use of the observed composite
score.

The PAG is consistent with an assignment/achievement connection in the
\FCIplus{} attrition panel, but the causal conclusion does not depend on that
endpoint. It depends on the experiment.

\subsection{Adding an aide does not show the same advantage}

The regular-with-aide contrasts are close to zero at kindergarten and grade 3,
and both intervals include zero. In these observed data there is no evidence
that adding an aide to a regular-size class reproduces the achievement
advantage of reducing class size.

\subsection{Early achievement strongly predicts later structure}

Kindergarten and grade-3 achievement remain adjacent in the full longitudinal
fits of all three algorithms and in 100\% of the self-implementation school
bootstraps. Kindergarten achievement is also adjacent to grade-3 observation
in all three attrition fits. Cross-implementation agreement therefore supports
a robust \emph{skeleton-level} relationship between early performance, later
performance, and later data availability.

This finding still does not quantify mediation. Kindergarten achievement is a
post-assignment variable, and a formal mediation analysis would require
additional counterfactual assumptions. Moreover, the direction of the
longitudinal achievement edge fails the chronological audit for both
\FCIplus{} implementations.

\subsection{Attrition is selective but not clearly caused by class arm}

The full-data attrition PAGs do not retain a direct class-arm/observation
adjacency, and its bootstrap frequency is low. Observation is more stably
connected to early achievement and measured context. The evidence therefore
supports concern about selective attrition without showing a stable direct
effect of class arm on grade-3 observation.

\section{What cannot be concluded}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
  \item \textbf{No unique DAG is identified.} Every PAG represents an
        equivalence class.
  \item \textbf{No causal effect magnitude comes from FCI.} The score
        differences are arm contrasts; PAG edges contain no ``points gained''
        coefficient.
  \item \textbf{A bidirected edge is not proof of a hidden confounder.} In the
        focused panel it conflicts with the known randomization unless
        selection, post-treatment conditioning, or other violations are
        considered.
  \item \textbf{The grade-3 contrast is not a complete long-run
        intention-to-treat estimate.} It conditions on observed grade-3
        outcomes.
  \item \textbf{Temporal reversals are not credible chronology.} They are
        algorithm diagnostics under violated or approximate assumptions.
  \item \textbf{Implementation agreement is not proof.} Shared CI decisions
        and shared logical rules can reproduce the same wrong endpoint.
  \item \textbf{Tested cyclic shifts are not all permutations.} Exact
        reproduction over 26 recorded cyclic orders is a finite audit, not a
        proof of general order invariance.
  \item \textbf{Bootstrap frequency is not a posterior probability.} It
        describes adjacency recurrence under school resampling and the fixed
        analysis pipeline.
  \item \textbf{Absence is policy conditional.} Failure to retain an edge
        under one node set, CI test, and threshold is not proof of zero causal
        effect.
\end{enumerate}

\keyresult{The strongest causal conclusion is design-based: smaller
kindergarten classes improve early observed achievement relative to regular
classes, while the aide arm shows no comparable effect. The main contribution
of the three-algorithm comparison is to separate replicated skeleton structure
from unstable endpoint orientation. It exposes selection, persistence, and
specification sensitivity around the experiment; it does not replace it.}

\chapter{Critical Self-Assessment}

\section{Strengths}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
  \item The algorithm and application are physically and logically separated.
  \item The data source, user guide, exact model inputs, random seeds, result
        tables, and report artifacts are committed.
  \item Standard FCI is always reported alongside \FCIplus{}.
  \item An independently maintained R implementation is run with matched CI
        semantics and its disagreements are retained in the report.
  \item Randomized-arm evidence is separated from exploratory PAG evidence.
  \item School-level resampling acknowledges the most important clustering
        unit.
  \item Endpoint reversals and threshold sensitivity are reported as failures
        of literal interpretation rather than silently corrected.
  \item The software is validated with exact oracle graphs, finite-sample
        integration tests, strict typing, multi-version tests, and wheel
        installation checks.
\end{itemize}

\section{Statistical limitations}

\subsection{Discretisation and sparse contingency tables}

Achievement and age are reduced to quantile categories, and teacher
experience is binned. This enables G-square testing across mixed educational
variables but discards within-bin information. As conditioning sets grow,
some multiway contingency cells may have small expected counts, weakening
the chi-square approximation.

\subsection{Dependence within schools and classrooms}

The primary CI test treats student rows as independent. The bootstrap
resamples schools, which measures sensitivity to school composition, but it
does not turn each underlying G-square test into a cluster-robust CI test.
Classroom identifiers and multilevel structure should be incorporated in a
more specialised analysis.

\subsection{Complete-case selection}

The longitudinal and focused panels require grade-3 scores. If observation is
affected by prior achievement, mobility, family circumstances, or school
processes, conditioning on complete cases may open selection paths. The
attrition panel provides evidence that observation is structurally related to
early variables, reinforcing this concern.

\subsection{Post-treatment variables}

Kindergarten achievement and teacher experience are measured during the
treatment period. They are not baseline covariates in the same sense as sex or
ethnicity. Including kindergarten achievement may block part of the
class-size pathway; excluding it may leave omitted post-treatment dependence.
The two panels deliberately show both specifications instead of selecting the
one with the preferred edge.

\subsection{Faithfulness and finite-sample CI error}

FCI-family guarantees rely on faithfulness and correct CI decisions. Near-zero
or cancelling effects may violate faithfulness, while finite samples produce
false dependence and false independence. The threshold-sensitive focused edge
is a direct example of this limitation.

\subsection{Unknown sparsity bound}

The \FCIplus{} paper profile uses \(k=3\). The true maximum degree of the
underlying STAR MAG is not observed and cannot be verified from the learned
graph alone. If the required separating set exceeds the bound, \FCIplus{} may
retain an extra edge. Conversely, \code{pcalg::fciPlus} does not expose a
directly comparable \(k\) argument, so the independent reference is not an
identical internal search schedule.

\subsection{Endpoint order sensitivity}

The R and paper-profile self-\FCIplus{} audits change some endpoint marks; the
paper self-\FCIplus{} longitudinal skeleton also changes slightly. This is a
practical warning for constraint-based orientation under finite-sample CI
decisions: deterministic output for one column order is not the same as
order-invariant causal information. The robust profile removes the observed
order dependence by stable deletion and conservative orientation. The report
therefore uses it for applied interpretation, while still giving more
evidential weight to skeleton consensus than to isolated endpoint marks.

\subsection{Coarse PAG bootstrap}

Twelve graph bootstrap replicates are computationally useful but statistically
coarse. Frequencies such as 83\% and 92\% correspond to ten and eleven
successful replicates. They should be viewed as stability indicators, not
precise probabilities that an edge is true.

\subsection{Software maturity and validation coverage}

The package is version 0.1.0 and classified pre-alpha.  Strict typing covers
the project's own interfaces, but third-party numerical and graph libraries
do not all provide equally complete type information.  Unit tests and
hand-authored oracle targets are strong regression evidence, not an
independent formal proof or an exhaustive MAG-equivalence checker.  In
particular, the complete Figure~4(b) PAG target is repository derived even
though its MAG and exhibited separator are source grounded.

\subsection{External-software comparability}

The known-truth comparison normalizes endpoint semantics and controls inputs,
but the interfaces do not expose identical search caps, query counters, or
timing boundaries.  The robust row also intentionally uses a different alpha
policy.  External agreement is therefore differential evidence, while
cross-language wall time is descriptive only.  Tetrad capability statements
were documentation checked and not executed.

\section{Substantive limitations}

\limitationbox{The STAR application demonstrates an auditable
causal-structure workflow; it is not a definitive re-estimation of the
experimental treatment effect, and known-impossible orientations are reported
as diagnostics.}

Original randomization blocks, class switching, compliance, and
school-specific assignment procedures are not reconstructed; a policy estimate
must return to those records and define an intention-to-treat estimand. The
transparent composite score simply adds reading and mathematics, leaving test
scaling, subject-specific, distributional, and subgroup effects unexamined.

\chapter{Conclusions and Future Work}

\section{Conclusions}

The project achieved its two-stage objective and answers the five research
questions with three bounded findings.

First, source fidelity is explicit and testable.  Standard FCI maps to the
historical 2000 search schedule, whose Theorem~6.4 is a correctness result for
a partially oriented inducing-path graph under correct CI information, not a
modern maximal-orientation theorem.  FCI+ maps to Claassen et al.'s augmented
skeleton and hierarchy in Algorithm~2; its polynomial result remains the
restricted \(O(N^{2(k+2)})\) exact-oracle query bound for fixed true degree
\(k\), faithfulness, and constant-time CI.  The source-to-code-to-test matrix
makes literal paper mechanisms, later orientation completeness, neutral
software structure, and robust engineering policy visibly different.

Second, validation is layered rather than circular. Exact m-separation tests
recover the independently specified targets on the encoded fixtures,
including hierarchical removal of the Figure~4(b) false link with the
source-exhibited separator \(\{U,V,Z\}\).  The known-truth benchmark executes
five methods over 15 runs: robust FCI+ improves the recorded mean endpoint
metrics, while paper FCI+ reduces logical queries relative to standard FCI.
The causal-learn and \code{pcalg} comparisons supply differential evidence,
and Tetrad remains documentation-only.  The package's defensible improvement
is its integrated audit surface, not a claim to be universally fastest or
most accurate.

Third, the package was applied to a real educational experiment without
mixing domain code into the algorithm. The STAR results show a clear observed
small-class achievement advantage, no comparable aide advantage, stable
early-to-later achievement structure, and evidence that later observation is
selective. An independent R \code{pcalg::fciPlus} run exactly replicates the
focused 10-edge PAG and broadly supports the other skeletons. It also shows
why PAGs require disciplined interpretation: the most tempting bidirected
treatment-outcome edge is conditional on complete cases and disappears under a
stricter alpha, while some endpoint marks change with variable order or violate
chronology.

A separate robust application profile addresses the principal empirical
failure found by those audits. Across every cyclic variable order it returns
the same PAG in all three panels, preserves the main target adjacencies, and
reports no fully directed temporal reversal. It does so by leaving unsupported
endpoints unresolved and by spending more CI calls. This is the appropriate
robustness trade-off for the application: fewer directional claims, backed by
stronger reproducibility over the tested orders. It is not proof of invariance
to every permutation.

No algorithm is uniformly ``best'' on the STAR data. The paper-profile
self-\FCIplus{} is the fidelity and query-efficiency subject. The robust
self-\FCIplus{} profile is preferred for this applied interpretation under
the predeclared order and temporal audits, although its circles identify less
direction. R \code{pcalg} provides
valuable independent replication and gives the most plausible direction for
the attrition target edge, yet shares the longitudinal reversal and exhibits
endpoint order sensitivity. Standard FCI is less economical but leaves the
longitudinal target partly unresolved rather than fully reversing it.
Credibility must therefore be assigned claim by claim.

The report therefore rejects two extremes. It is too pessimistic to conclude
that causal discovery adds nothing to a randomized study; the attrition and
stability analysis reveals useful structure. It is too optimistic to conclude
that FCI has rediscovered and fully identified the experiment's causal model.
The correct contribution lies between them: an auditable map of conditional
independence constraints, uncertainty, and modelling sensitivity.

\section{Recommended extensions}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em,itemsep=0.25em,parsep=0pt,topsep=0.3em]
  \item Reconstruct the randomization strata, estimate formal intention-to-treat
        effects, and model grade-3 observation explicitly.
  \item Develop cluster-aware or multilevel CI tests for students nested within
        classrooms and schools.
  \item Increase PAG bootstrap repetitions and report Monte Carlo uncertainty
        for stability frequencies.
  \item Run separate reading and mathematics analyses and examine prespecified
        treatment-effect heterogeneity.
  \item Evaluate defensible temporal background knowledge separately from the
        data-only robust profile, without silently forcing preferred arrows.
  \item Compare alternative CI tests and \(k=2,3,4\) \FCIplus{} profiles while
        diagnosing which edges depend on the sparsity bound.
  \item Add an independent exhaustive MAG-equivalence oracle for target-PAG
        derivation and extend stability reporting to endpoint compatibility.
\end{enumerate}

\chapter{Researcher Interpretation Checklist}

Before treating an FCI-family result as substantive causal evidence, the
following questions should be answered.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
  \item What is the estimand: a graph, an ancestry claim, or a causal effect?
  \item Which variables are pre-treatment, post-treatment, outcomes, or
        selection indicators?
  \item Does the chosen CI test match the data type and missingness pattern?
  \item Are observations independent, or is clustering present?
  \item Is the \FCIplus{} sparsity bound plausible and sensitivity-tested?
  \item Does standard FCI recover the same important skeleton relations?
  \item Are adjacencies stable under resampling?
  \item Are endpoints stable under alpha, binning, and variable-set changes?
  \item Do any directed edges violate known temporal order or randomization?
  \item Is a bidirected edge being over-interpreted as proof of a specific
        latent variable?
  \item Are design-based conclusions being kept separate from data-only PAG
        conclusions?
  \item Can every published number be regenerated from committed inputs?
\end{enumerate}
