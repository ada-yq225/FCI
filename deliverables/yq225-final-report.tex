\begin{abstract}
\noindent
This project independently implements the FCI and \FCIplus{} causal-discovery
algorithms, validates them against exact graphical oracles and established
software, and applies them to the Tennessee STAR class-size experiment. FCI
learns partial ancestral graphs (PAGs) that remain valid under latent
confounding and selection, but its Possible-D-SEP search is expensive;
\FCIplus{} attains a polynomial conditional-independence (CI) query bound for
sparse graphs. Both search schedules are implemented in a self-contained,
fully typed Python package (9,559 lines, 38 modules, no causal-discovery
dependencies), validated by 367 automated tests, exact m-separation fixtures,
a 15-run known-truth benchmark, and differential comparisons with causal-learn
and R \code{pcalg}. On the benchmark, a robust application profile of
\FCIplus{} attains mean skeleton F1 0.985 and endpoint accuracy 0.840, against
0.977 and 0.737 for paper-profile FCI. The separate STAR application (6,325
kindergarten-cohort students) finds a design-supported small-class advantage
of 13.90 points in observed kindergarten achievement (school-cluster 95\%
interval 5.21 to 22.07) and near-zero aide contrasts; because the outcome
subset is post-assignment selected, this is not an identified causal effect.
Across methods, skeleton-level structure proves more defensible than several
order-, threshold-, or chronology-sensitive endpoint marks; no PAG here
identifies a unique DAG or estimates a treatment effect.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Many empirical studies ask causal questions where not every common cause has
been measured. Family background, institutional selection, and administrative
recording can generate dependence that no causally sufficient DAG over the
recorded columns represents honestly; fitting one anyway turns uncertainty
into unjustified arrows.

FCI was designed for this problem: under causal Markov, faithfulness, and
appropriate sampling assumptions, it learns from conditional-independence
(CI) information a Partial Ancestral Graph (PAG) that remains valid when
latent confounders and selection variables may exist
\citep{spirtes2000causation,richardson2002ancestral,glymour2019review}. The
price is extra search and endpoint ambiguity: the Possible-D-SEP refinement
may test many separating sets outside local neighbourhoods.
\citet{claassen2013learning}
address the search cost for sparse graphs, proving a polynomial worst-case
CI-query bound for a fixed maximum degree \(k\).

This project independently implements paper-aligned FCI and \FCIplus{},
validates them against exact graphical oracles and established software, and
separates algorithm fidelity from a robust finite-sample application
workflow, delivered as four auditable contributions.

\begin{description}[leftmargin=2.4em,itemsep=0.15em,topsep=0.2em,
  font=\normalfont\bfseries\color{ReportNavy}]
\item[C1.] \emph{Algorithm implementation.} A self-contained, fully typed
Python package (\code{fci\_engine}), written from the primary sources without
depending on any causal-discovery library: five CI tests, an explicit PAG
endpoint algebra, the ordered Possible-D-SEP stage, the complete Algorithm~2
stage, and an orientation engine with five selectable rule schedules.
\item[C2.] \emph{Validation apparatus.} An exact MAG m-separation oracle,
encoded published reference fixtures, a 15-run known-truth benchmark, and 367
automated tests executed under strict typing on Python 3.9--3.13.
\item[C3.] \emph{Software positioning.} Executed differential comparisons
against causal-learn and R \code{pcalg} under matched CI semantics. Among
interfaces checked on 26 July 2026, no other Python implementation of
Claassen's \FCIplus{} with paper-profile control and a structured audit trail
was identified.
\item[C4.] \emph{Application.} A separate Tennessee STAR case study (plus an
independent R replication runner) that consumes only the public API, with
school-cluster bootstrap, cyclic-order, chronology, and sensitivity audits
organised by a pre-specified evidence hierarchy.
\end{description}

The separation between C1 and C4 is central: an algorithm should remain
reusable and domain-neutral, whereas an application must state its own coding,
selection, clustering, and interpretation decisions.

Five questions guided the work. \textbf{RQ1}: how do the sources define their
targets, assumptions, and guarantee boundaries? \textbf{RQ2}: does the
implementation map those stages to explicit code and tests? \textbf{RQ3}: what
is recovered under exact-oracle and finite-sample known-truth conditions?
\textbf{RQ4}: how does the package compare with established FCI software?
\textbf{RQ5}: which STAR conclusions are design based, robust at the skeleton
level, unresolved at the endpoint level, or merely exploratory?
\Cref{sec:background,sec:implementation} address theory and implementation,
\cref{sec:validation,sec:software} controlled evidence, and
\cref{sec:star-design,sec:star-results,sec:interpretation} the application;
this ordering prevents a real-data pattern from serving as evidence that the
algorithm matches its sources.

\section{Background and Source Algorithms}
\label{sec:background}

\subsection{Graphs, assumptions, and PAGs}

Constraint-based discovery begins from statements \(X \perp Y \mid Z\) over
observed variables \(V\), with latent variables \(L\) and selection variables
\(S\) possibly present. The \emph{causal Markov condition} states that
graphical separation implies conditional independence; \emph{faithfulness}
states that observed independences arise from separation rather than exact
cancellation. Under causal sufficiency these support DAG methods such as PC.
Otherwise the appropriate observed-variable object is a maximal ancestral
graph (MAG) \citep{richardson2002ancestral}: marginalising \(L\) and
conditioning on \(S\) induce directed, bidirected, and undirected relations,
and every missing MAG edge corresponds to an m-separation. Because different
MAGs can encode the same independence model, the identifiable output is a
PAG, recording exactly the endpoint features shared by one Markov equivalence
class \citep{zhang2008completeness}.

Each PAG adjacency carries two endpoint marks: a circle leaves ancestry
unresolved, an arrowhead at \(Y\) excludes \(Y\) as an ancestor of \(X\), a
tail asserts ancestry in every represented MAG, a bidirected edge is
compatible with latent confounding without identifying a unique latent
variable, and undirected edges reflect selection structure. An adjacency says
only that the tested sets failed to separate the pair; neither an adjacency
nor an endpoint alone identifies a structural coefficient, an adjustment set,
or an interventional contrast \citep{pearl2009causality}. M-separation
generalises d-separation and is the criterion used by this project's exact
oracle; sample tests are error-prone, so oracle recovery isolates graph logic
rather than finite-sample accuracy.

\subsection{Standard FCI and its historical boundary}

FCI does not require that all common causes of measured variables are
measured \citep[Ch.~6]{spirtes2000causation}. A PC-style search removes
\(X - Y\) when some subset of current local neighbours separates the pair.
With latent variables, a pair may remain adjacent even though it is
separable, because a necessary separator can contain a node no longer
adjacent to either endpoint. The book introduces D-SEP to characterise where
an observed separator can reside and, since D-SEP is not computable from a
partially learned graph, the observable superset Possible-D-SEP: for an
ordered pair \((A,B)\), a node \(V \neq A\) qualifies when some path from
\(A\) to \(V\) has every internal triple either a collider or a triangle
whose middle node is
not a definite noncollider. The construction is deliberately permissive and is
required \emph{separately} from each ordered endpoint: using one direction
only, or mixing the two pools, changes the search contract. The full
schedule: PC-style adjacency search; initial unshielded colliders; ordered
Possible-D-SEP refinement; endpoint reset and collider reconstruction from
final separators; orientation closure. Theorem 6.4 guarantees a
correct \emph{partially oriented inducing path graph} under correct
population-level CI decisions --- while the authors state they did not know
whether the output was maximally informative. Complete orientation therefore
depends on the later rules of \citet{zhang2008completeness} and is not
retroactively attributed to the 2000 source.

\subsection{\FCIplus{}: Algorithm 2}

\citet{claassen2013learning} ask whether causal discovery remains
intrinsically exponential when the observed structure is sparse. Their task
assumes an independence oracle answering any query exactly in constant time,
a distribution faithful to a causal DAG, an observed-variable MAG with
maximum degree at most a
constant \(k\), and a completed PAG as output. Under those assumptions they
prove the worst-case CI-query bound \(O(N^{2(k+2)})\) for \(N\) observed
variables --- narrower than the paper's title: it bounds oracle queries and
does not make a statistical test exact or constant-time.

A nonadjacent pair in the true MAG can survive bounded adjacency search when
every useful separator requires a node outside the endpoint-adjacency union;
such a false adjacency is a \emph{D-sep link}. Rather than searching a large
Possible-D-SEP pool,
\FCIplus{} extracts invariant non-ancestry information into an
\emph{augmented skeleton} \(G^{+}\): when a known minimal independence
\(X \perp Y \mid S\) becomes a dependence given \(S \cup \{A\}\) for a
suitable adjacent node \(A\), an invariant arrowhead at \(A\) is supported. In
\(G^{+}\), a D-sep link \(X - Y\) appears as the middle edge of
\(U \leftrightarrow X \leftrightarrow Y \leftrightarrow V\) with \(U\), \(V\)
nonadjacent and the required cross-direction paths not running against an
arrowhead; the pattern only \emph{proposes} a candidate, and independence
evidence decides deletion. Conditioning sets come from the recursive
hierarchy \(\mathrm{HIE}(X, I)\), the fixed point of repeatedly adding
recorded minimal separators of members; because candidates can depend on one
another, each successful removal rebuilds the augmented skeleton and
candidate list (\cref{fig:workflow}). The arXiv proof supplement's
abstract states \(N^{2(k+1)}\) while its theorem and proof agree with the UAI
paper's \(N^{2(k+2)}\), a discrepancy recorded rather than harmonised.
Algorithm~2 invokes complete FCI orientation rather than rederiving it, so
its completed-output claim inherits \citet{zhang2008completeness} as a
dependency.

\subsection{What the sources do not establish}

Neither source guarantees finite-sample recovery: both theorems are
conditional on correct CI decisions. Neither identifies a unique DAG, an
adjustment set, or a treatment effect; a bidirected pattern does not name or
measure a hidden cause. The 2000 source proves no maximal orientation and no
polynomial bound; the 2013 source proves no finite-sample superiority and
nothing when \(k\) is wrong --- a user-entered bound is not evidence --- and
its authors warn that hierarchy-derived conditioning sets may be large,
reducing finite-sample power.

\section{Independent Python Implementation}
\label{sec:implementation}

\subsection{Package boundary and public API}

The objective is to expose the algorithmic obligations of
\cref{sec:background} as inspectable components, separate from finite-sample
application policy, rather than to relabel a generic constraint-based search
as \FCIplus{}. Reusable code resides in \code{src/fci\_engine}, the STAR
workflow in \code{case\_studies/tennessee\_star}; no STAR-specific choice is
embedded in the estimator.

The two algorithms are separate entry points with explicit paper profiles, so
a default optimised for finite samples cannot silently stand in for the
source algorithm. \code{FCIConfig.paper()} fixes unbounded depth and
path length, immediate deletion, first-found separators, enabled
Possible-D-SEP, nonconservative colliders, and the historical
\code{spirtes\_2000} closure. \code{FCIPlusConfig.paper(k)} requires an
explicit \(k\), assigns it to both the conditioning depth and the
endpoint-base sparsity bound, disables Possible-D-SEP, and selects the
complete \code{standard} closure required by Algorithm~2's output claim.
Profiles constrain execution; they do not validate theorem assumptions ---
supplying \(k=3\) does not prove the true MAG has degree at most three.

The \code{PAG} class stores an endpoint mark at each side of every adjacency,
keeping tails, arrowheads, circles, and absence explicit. Every search uses
one \code{CITest.test(data, x, y, cond\_set)} contract, with built-in
Fisher-Z, missing-value Fisher-Z, chi-square, G-square, and kernel tests. A
cache canonicalises each query, so results separate the \emph{logical} CI
requests made by control flow from cache hits and executed statistics --- the
distinction relevant to a theorem that counts oracle queries.

\subsection{Pipelines, orientation schedules, and result objects}

\code{FCI.fit} exposes the source stage ordering directly, recording
Possible-D-SEP removals with source \code{pdsep}. \code{FCIPlus.fit}
deliberately does not call Possible-D-SEP; it passes the initial skeleton to
the Algorithm~2 search: augmented skeleton, bidirected witness patterns,
paired endpoint bases, hierarchy fixed points, separator testing and
minimisation, graph update with source \code{fci\_plus\_dsep}, and candidate
rebuild. A
diagnostics record counts candidates, revisits, hierarchy queries,
D-SEP-stage CI requests, and the maximum conditioning size --- empirical
accounting that makes a run inspectable, not a mechanised proof of the
bound.

One dispatcher provides five orientation schedules: \code{spirtes\_2000}, the
historical closure used by the standard-FCI paper profile; \code{standard},
the modern complete schedule required by the \FCIplus{} paper profile; and
\code{conservative}, \code{leaf}, and \code{robust}, finite-sample
engineering policies supporting no completed-PAG claim. A dedicated
regression verifies that \code{spirtes\_2000} stops before a selection-bias
tail completion that \code{standard} can orient, making the historical
completeness boundary executable rather than descriptive.

\code{FCIResult} binds the final graph to the evidence that produced it ---
resolved configuration, separators with stage sources, logical CI counts,
named CI and orientation traces, and diagnostics; \code{explain\_edge}
returns every decision behind a pair, and \code{save\_artifacts} exports
JSON, CSV, and HTML. A plotted arrow therefore cannot become detached from
the profile, test, threshold, and rule that created it.

\subsection{Engineering scope and application policy}

The committed code base was written entirely for this project: the algorithm
package spans 9,559 lines across 38 typed modules; the test suite contains
367 tests in 44 modules (5,929 lines); the STAR application adds 3,052 lines
plus a 396-line independent R runner; benchmark and figure scripts bring the
total to roughly 25,300. The package ships \code{py.typed} and passes Ruff,
strict MyPy, a wheel build, and Pytest on Python 3.9--3.13; a full
concept-to-symbol-to-test traceability matrix is maintained in the
repository's extended report. These checks establish
interface and packaging consistency, not faithfulness, true sparsity, or any
empirical causal conclusion.

The practical profile trades computation for stability: stable deletion
reduces variable-order dependence; max-\(p\) separator selection spends more
CI queries to retain stronger independence evidence; conservative collider
and tail policies preserve circles where evidence is ambiguous; automatic
\(\alpha\) is a documented sample-size heuristic. Accordingly, ``paper
\FCIplus{}'' is the fidelity and query-efficiency subject, and ``robust
\FCIplus{}'' is the preferred policy for the reported application because it
meets the stated audit criteria while returning less-specific endpoints --- a
local preference, not a universal ranking.

\section{Algorithm Validation}
\label{sec:validation}

\subsection{Exact oracle tests}

The strongest tests replace sample CI decisions with exact m-separation in a
known MAG, isolating search correctness from sampling error. Three
independently specified targets come from published structures: the canonical Figure~4(b) D-SEP example of
\citet{claassen2013learning}; a latent-variable reference graph from the FCI
literature; and an orientation-rule reference graph associated with
\citet{zhang2008completeness}. Both FCI and \FCIplus{} recover the specified
PAGs on all three fixtures, and Figure~4(b) is additionally tested under four
variable orders.

The Figure~4(b) MAG has six edges (\(Z \rightarrow U\),
\(Z \rightarrow V\), \(U \leftrightarrow X\), \(V \rightarrow X\),
\(U \rightarrow Y\), \(V \leftrightarrow Y\)); the adjacency stage retains a
false \(X - Y\) link although \(X \perp Y \mid \{U,V,Z\}\), with \(Z\)
adjacent to neither endpoint. The source exhibits this separator but prints
no endpoint-complete PAG, so the completed comparison target is repository
derived (\cref{fig:figure4}). On this example, paper-aligned standard FCI
needs 102 CI queries and \FCIplus{} 63 (a 38.2\% reduction), both returning
the exact target; the values are frozen in
\code{reports/data/oracle\_validation\_summary.csv} with the target hash and
separator provenance.

\subsection{Finite-sample behaviour}

Real data replace exact answers with noisy decisions. On a seeded latent
linear SEM over the same reference graph, at \(N = 5{,}000\) \FCIplus{}
returns an extra \(X \leftrightarrow Y\) (exact-edge F1 0.923) while standard
FCI recovers the exact PAG; at \(N = 50{,}000\) both are exact. This is a
reproducible fixture, not a universal sample-size threshold.

The application safeguard was tested on 15 deterministic latent-SEM cases:
five realistic graph families, three seeds each, 2,500 observations per case,
with a known oracle PAG (\cref{tab:robust-finite-sample}).

and can make different errors.

\keyresult{The oracle tests support the correctness of the implemented
search-and-orientation pipelines under exact CI information; the
finite-sample tests show empirical results remain limited by CI-test error
and tuning choices.}

\section{Comparison with Established Software}
\label{sec:software}

The comparison separates execution from interface inspection: the executed
layer asks what happened on identical committed data under recorded settings;
the capability layer asks what each public interface or documentation
exposes. Tetrad 7.6.10 \citep{tetrad2026} appears only in the latter,
documentation-only, because no working Java runtime was available in the
recorded environment. All claims are dated 26 July 2026.

The benchmark reuses the 15 known-truth runs above, with every method
receiving the same numeric matrix and variable order. Fixed paper and

Exact F1 requires both endpoint marks to match; semantic F1 additionally
credits endpoint patterns compatible with the target ancestral constraints;
endpoint accuracy scores individual marks. The common conversion represents
each edge as an unordered pair plus two semantic marks, never as generic
directed edges. The equality of the aggregate local paper-\FCIplus{},
causal-learn \citep{causallearn2026}, and \code{pcalg}
\citep{kalisch2012pcalg} scores is useful differential evidence, not an
equivalence proof: local and causal-learn times cover in-process calls
whereas the R timing includes process startup, and \code{pcalg::fciPlus}
exposes no identical control for the degree bound \(k\). Throughout, data,
order, alpha, CI semantics, and search caps are held fixed where interfaces
allow and every mismatch is disclosed; distinct FCI-family algorithms are
never relabelled as one; logical queries, executed tests, and wall time
remain different quantities.

On the capability layer, \code{pcalg} exposes both algorithms and accepts
user-defined CI tests, but its \code{fciPlus} result carries materially less
separator provenance; causal-learn exposes standard FCI but no public
Claassen \FCIplus{} entry point; Tetrad provides FCI-family hybrids, none
established as a drop-in Claassen \FCIplus{}. The
local package integrates paper-profile control, structured traces, separator
provenance, diagnostics, per-edge explanations, and stability auditing in one
workflow --- not found as a single public workflow in the checked interfaces;
an auditability improvement, not a universal accuracy or speed claim.

\section{STAR Study Design and Methods}
\label{sec:star-design}

\subsection{Experiment, cohort, and panels}

The Tennessee Student/Teacher Achievement Ratio project was a large
randomized experiment in kindergarten through grade 3, assigning students and
teachers to small classes (about 13--17 students), regular classes (about
22--25), or regular classes with a full-time aide \citep{word1990star}; it
remains a central source of evidence on class size
\citep{krueger1999experimental}. This analysis uses the official Harvard
Dataverse release \citep{achilles2008star}: 11,601 student records, 379
variables, CC0 licence, with codes checked against the accompanying 146-page
user guide.

Randomization already provides a stronger basis for estimating the class-size
effect than structure learning, so FCI is not used to rediscover it. The
purposes are: whether grade-3 observation is structurally related to early
achievement or background; whether early and later achievement remain
adjacent given measured context; how the PAG changes when an early
post-assignment outcome is included or excluded; whether the implementations
agree; and how much search work \FCIplus{} saves. The design also supplies an
external audit: a data-only PAG implying an implausible pre-treatment
direction, or latent confounding of a randomized assignment, should be
reported as a discrepancy rather than accepted literally.

The analysis covers the 6,325 students with a kindergarten class assignment,
across 79 kindergarten schools, arranged into three panels that are
deliberately not pooled because each encodes a different statistical question
(\cref{tab:panels}). All model inputs are integer-coded discrete categories:
class arm, sex, ethnicity (small source categories collapsed), free-lunch
status, school context, entry-age quantiles, binned teacher experience, and
reading-plus-mathematics scaled scores converted to achievement quartiles;
the exact numeric tables supplied to the algorithms are committed in the
repository.

\subsection{Analysis protocol}

Before discovery, observed means and randomized-arm contrasts are computed
for kindergarten scores, grade-3 scores among students with both scores, and
the probability that both grade-3 scores are observed, with uncertainty from
1,000 bootstrap samples of kindergarten schools, preserving within-school
dependence; these design-based contrasts are reported separately from
discovery output.

Discovery uses the likelihood-ratio G-square test,
\(G^2 = 2 \sum_s \sum_{x,y} O_{xys} \log ( O_{xys} / E_{xys} )\), at
\(\alpha = 0.05\). Standard FCI runs its unbounded paper schedule; paper
\FCIplus{} uses \(k = 3\); robust \FCIplus{} uses the practical profile
(stable skeleton, strongest separating sets, conservative colliders, robust
orientation, depth and sparsity bound 3); the independent reference is
\code{pcalg::fciPlus} with selection bias enabled and no exposed \(k\). No
temporal priors are supplied to discovery; reversals are audited after
fitting.

Three audits probe stability: 100 school-cluster bootstrap refits per
algorithm and panel, recording each unordered adjacency's appearance
frequency (an exploratory diagnostic, not a confidence interval or posterior
probability); a cyclic-order audit refitting every cyclic shift of the
variable list for all profiles and the R reference; and a focused-panel
sensitivity grid over \(\alpha \in \{0.01, 0.05\}\), three versus four
quantile bins, and \(k \in \{2,3,4\}\).

The application distinguishes a reproducible adjacency from a credible
direction: a skeleton-level conclusion is promoted only when the adjacency
survives every cyclic shift with high bootstrap frequency; a directional
conclusion additionally requires invariant endpoint marks, no conflict with
measurement time or randomization, and no material reversal under sensitivity
checks. Failure downgrades the claim to unresolved direction rather than
deleting the edge.

\subsection{Attrition and longitudinal panels}

On the attrition panel, the three implementations substantially agree at the
skeleton level (\cref{fig:pag-attrition}; edge counts, agreement, and search
cost in \cref{tab:agreement-performance}). All three omit a direct
\code{K\_Class}--\code{Grade3\_Observed} adjacency, which appears in only
12--16\% of school bootstraps. By contrast,
\code{K\_Achievement}--\code{Grade3\_Observed} is adjacent in 100\% of
bootstrap fits for all three local profiles and the free-lunch/observation
adjacency is also stable: later availability is not independent of early
achievement and measured context. The three endpoint
readings diverge: standard FCI gives the impossible \emph{grade-3 observation
\(\rightarrow\) kindergarten achievement}; self-\FCIplus{} leaves a circle;
R-\FCIplus{} gives the temporally admissible direction. The R reading is more
plausible, but package identity cannot establish statistical correctness. The
attrition PAG also does not prove a missingness mechanism; a formal analysis
would require an explicit estimand and additional assumptions.

On the longitudinal panel, all three implementations retain the
kindergarten/grade-3 achievement adjacency, present in all 100 bootstrap

class-assignment/grade-3 adjacency once kindergarten achievement is included,
yet that adjacency reappears in 71\% of FCI, 79\% of paper \FCIplus{}, and
80\% of robust-profile bootstrap fits --- a warning that conservative
orientation and order invariance do not remove school-resampling sensitivity.
Both \FCIplus{} implementations orient grade-3 achievement toward
kindergarten achievement, which is temporally impossible if read causally,
while standard FCI leaves that endpoint unresolved. Agreement between
implementations is not sufficient to override chronology: both may replicate
the same finite-sample CI pattern under complete-case selection.

\subsection{Focused treatment-outcome panel}

Removing kindergarten achievement changes the result: all three algorithms
return the same 10-edge PAG --- not merely the same skeleton --- including
\(\mathit{K\_Class} \leftrightarrow \mathit{Grade3\_Achievement}\)
(\cref{fig:pag-focused}), an adjacency appearing in 92\%, 85\%, and 96\% of
FCI, paper, and robust bootstraps. Independent exact
replication makes this conditional-independence pattern more credible, but it
does not make a literal latent-confounding reading credible: kindergarten
class assignment was randomized, while the focused panel conditions on
grade-3 outcomes being observed and omits early post-assignment achievement.
Selection on observation, school-level dependence, omitted post-randomization
variables, and CI-test error can all produce arrowheads at both endpoints.

\section{Interpretation, Evidence Hierarchy, and Limitations}
\label{sec:interpretation}

\subsection{Evidence hierarchy}

An explicit hierarchy prevents a visually definite PAG mark from outranking
the experimental design. Tier 1: randomized assignment with the
school-cluster contrast, permitting a bounded causal statement for the
analysed observed outcome. Tier 2: an adjacency replicated across methods,
resamples, orders, and sensitivity settings --- a robust skeleton-level claim
with no magnitude or direction. Tier 3: a stable skeleton with circles or
endpoint disagreement, where adjacency is more credible than orientation.
Tier 4: an implementation-, order-, or chronology-sensitive endpoint ---
diagnostic evidence only. Tier 5: a threshold-sensitive or complete-case
exploratory pattern --- a hypothesis for external validation.

\subsection{What can and cannot be concluded}

Within the kindergarten cohort and among students with observed score
components, the small-class arm is 13.90 points above the regular arm
(school-cluster 95\% interval 5.21 to 22.07) --- consistent with a beneficial
early effect, but not an identified causal effect in the post-assignment
observed subset without assumptions about missing outcomes, compliance, and
the original randomization blocks. The aide contrasts are close to zero at
both time points: no evidence that adding an aide reproduces the advantage of
reducing class size. Kindergarten and grade-3 achievement remain adjacent in
all full longitudinal fits and in 100\% of school bootstraps, and
kindergarten achievement is adjacent to grade-3 observation in all attrition
fits: a robust \emph{skeleton-level} relationship between early performance,
later performance, and later availability. The full-data attrition PAGs do
not retain a direct class-arm/observation adjacency and its bootstrap
frequency is low, supporting concern about selective attrition without a
stable direct effect of arm on observation.

Conversely: no unique DAG is identified --- every PAG represents an
equivalence class --- and no effect magnitude comes from FCI. A bidirected
edge is not proof of a hidden confounder; in the focused panel it conflicts
with the known randomization unless selection or post-treatment conditioning
is considered. Temporal reversals are algorithm diagnostics, not credible
chronology. Implementation agreement is not proof: shared CI decisions and
rules can reproduce the same wrong endpoint. The 26 recorded shifts are a
finite audit, not a proof of order invariance; bootstrap frequency is not a
posterior probability. Absence is policy conditional: failure to retain an
edge under one node set, test, and threshold is not proof of zero effect.

\subsection{Limitations}

Achievement and age are reduced to quantile categories and teacher experience
is binned, which enables G-square testing but discards within-bin information
and can leave small expected counts as conditioning sets grow. The CI test
treats student rows as independent; the school bootstrap measures sensitivity
to school composition but does not make each G-square test cluster-robust.
The longitudinal and focused panels require grade-3 scores, so conditioning
on complete cases may open selection paths --- a concern reinforced by the
attrition panel itself. Kindergarten achievement is measured during the
treatment period, so including it may block part of the class-size pathway
while excluding it may leave omitted post-treatment dependence; both
specifications are reported rather than selecting the one with the preferred
edge. FCI-family guarantees rely on faithfulness and correct CI decisions,
and the threshold-sensitive focused edge is a direct example of that
limitation. The true maximum degree of the STAR MAG is unobserved, so
\(k = 3\) is a bounded analysis setting rather than a verified assumption.
The package is version 0.1.0; hand-authored oracle targets are strong
regression evidence, not a formal proof or an exhaustive MAG-equivalence
check.

A deterministic scaling audit (5 to 80 nodes; a directed chain and the
Figure~4(b) core with isolated variables added) recovers the exact target
skeleton in every run but shows the query advantage is not universal:
\FCIplus{} wins on the five-node core (62 versus 101 queries) yet spends
5,162 versus 3,251 at 80 nodes, and 24,338 versus 12,638 on the 80-node
chain --- consistent with a worst-case bound; augmentation has a real cost
when targeted D-SEP search offers little benefit. Substantively, original randomization blocks, class switching,
compliance, and school-specific assignment procedures are not reconstructed,
and the composite score simply adds reading and mathematics, leaving scaling,
subject-specific, and subgroup effects unexamined.

\section{Conclusions and Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusions}

Source fidelity is explicit and testable. Standard FCI maps to the historical
2000 schedule, whose Theorem 6.4 is a correctness result under correct CI
information, not a modern maximal-orientation theorem; \FCIplus{} maps to the
augmented skeleton and hierarchy of Algorithm~2, whose polynomial result
remains the restricted \(O(N^{2(k+2)})\) exact-oracle query bound. Validation
is layered rather than circular: exact m-separation tests recover
independently specified targets, including the Figure~4(b) false-link
removal; the known-truth benchmark executes five methods over 15 runs, where
robust \FCIplus{} improves endpoint metrics and paper \FCIplus{} reduces
queries; the causal-learn and \code{pcalg} comparisons supply differential
evidence. The package's defensible improvement is its integrated
audit surface, not a claim to be universally fastest or most accurate.

Applied to STAR without mixing domain code into the algorithm, the workflow
shows a clear observed small-class advantage, no comparable aide advantage,
stable early-to-later achievement structure, and evidence that later
observation is selective. An independent R run exactly replicates the focused
10-edge PAG and broadly supports the other skeletons, while showing why PAGs
require discipline: the most tempting bidirected treatment-outcome edge is
conditional on complete cases and disappears under a stricter alpha, and some
endpoint marks change with order or violate chronology. The robust profile
addresses the principal failures found by those audits --- identical output
across every tested order, preserved target adjacencies, no directed temporal
reversal --- at the cost of unresolved endpoints and more CI calls.

No algorithm is uniformly best on these data: paper self-\FCIplus{} is the
fidelity and query-efficiency subject; robust self-\FCIplus{} is preferred
for applied interpretation under the predeclared audits; R \code{pcalg}
provides independent replication and the most plausible attrition direction
yet shares the longitudinal reversal; standard FCI is less economical but
leaves that target unresolved rather than reversed. Credibility must be assigned claim by claim. The report rejects two
extremes: too pessimistic to conclude that causal discovery adds nothing to a
randomized study --- the attrition and stability analysis reveals useful
structure --- and too optimistic to conclude that FCI has rediscovered the
experiment's causal model. The contribution lies between: an auditable map of
conditional-independence constraints, uncertainty, and modelling
sensitivity.

Recommended extensions are to reconstruct the randomization strata and
estimate formal intention-to-treat effects with an explicit model for grade-3
observation; develop cluster-aware CI tests; extend stability summaries to
endpoint compatibility; analyse reading and mathematics separately; evaluate
defensible temporal background knowledge separately from the data-only robust
profile; and add an exhaustive MAG-equivalence oracle for target-PAG
derivation.
