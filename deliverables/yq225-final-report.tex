\begin{abstract}
\noindent
This project presents an independent, auditable implementation of FCI and
\FCIplus{}, validates the two algorithms, and applies them to the Tennessee
STAR class-size experiment. FCI learns Partial Ancestral Graphs (PAGs) in the
presence of latent confounding and selection, although its Possible-D-SEP
search is computationally demanding; for sparse graphs, \FCIplus{} instead
admits a polynomial bound on conditional-independence (CI) queries. The
resulting fully typed Python package has no causal-discovery dependency and
provides both paper-aligned execution schedules and structured decision
traces. Validation draws on 367 automated tests, three exact m-separation
targets, a 15-run known-truth benchmark, and differential comparisons with
causal-learn and R \code{pcalg}. Robust \FCIplus{} achieves a mean skeleton
F1 of 0.985 and endpoint accuracy of 0.840, compared with 0.977 and 0.737 for
paper-profile FCI. Among 6,325 students in the STAR kindergarten cohort,
small classes are associated with a 13.90-point observed kindergarten
advantage (school-cluster 95\% interval 5.21--22.07), while aide contrasts
remain close to zero. Because the analysed outcomes are selected after
assignment, this contrast is not an identified treatment effect. Across
methods, skeleton relations are more defensible than endpoints that vary
with ordering, threshold, or chronology; none of the learned PAGs identifies
a unique DAG or an effect magnitude.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

\paragraph{Problem description.}
Many empirical studies pose causal questions even though some common causes
remain unmeasured. Family background, institutional selection, and
administrative recording can induce dependencies that cannot be faithfully
represented by a causally sufficient DAG over the recorded variables;
nevertheless fitting such a DAG converts genuine uncertainty into
unsupported arrows. FCI is designed for precisely this setting. Under the
causal Markov condition, faithfulness, and appropriate sampling assumptions,
it uses conditional-independence (CI) information to learn a Partial
Ancestral Graph (PAG) that accommodates latent confounding and selection
\citep{spirtes2000causation,richardson2002ancestral,glymour2019review}.
This flexibility comes with an expensive Possible-D-SEP search and ambiguous
endpoints. For sparse graphs with fixed maximum degree \(k\),
\citet{claassen2013learning} establish a polynomial worst-case bound on the
number of CI queries.

This leads to two research questions. First, can FCI and \FCIplus{} be
reimplemented from their primary sources in a faithful and verifiable way?
Second, what conclusions can that implementation legitimately support when
applied to the Tennessee STAR class-size experiment, a real randomized
educational study whose observed data are affected by attrition and
selection?

\paragraph{Significance.}
Public FCI-family tools generally provide limited visibility into the
evidence behind oriented endpoints. Among the interfaces examined in
\cref{sec:background}, none offers a Python implementation of Claassen's
\FCIplus{} with paper-profile control and a structured audit trail. Linking
each graph mark to recorded CI decisions supports defensible structural
claims and differential testing. STAR also provides a canonical application
in which selective attrition complicates longitudinal inference.

\paragraph{Objectives and contributions.}
Four measurable objectives address the auditability and finite-sample gaps
identified above, with each objective also defining a contribution of the
project.

\begin{description}[leftmargin=2.1em,itemsep=0.1em,topsep=0.2em,
  font=\normalfont\bfseries]
\item[O1/C1.] \emph{Implement source-faithful algorithms.} Success requires
separate paper profiles whose stages map to primary-source concepts, explicit
PAG endpoint semantics, typed public APIs, and no causal-discovery dependency.
The delivered 9,559-line, 38-module package supplies five CI tests, ordered
Possible-D-SEP, complete Algorithm~2 stages, and five orientation schedules.
\item[O2/C2.] \emph{Verify and validate.} Success requires exact recovery of
all pre-specified oracle targets, finite-sample accuracy reporting, and a clean
automated test matrix. The apparatus comprises an exact MAG m-separation
oracle, three published-structure targets, 15 known-truth runs, and 367 tests
under strict typing on Python 3.9--3.13.
\item[O3/C3.] \emph{Position against established software.} Success requires
matched-input comparisons with disclosed interface mismatches, using skeleton,
endpoint, runtime, and CI-query metrics. Executed causal-learn and R
\code{pcalg} runners provide this evidence.
\item[O4/C4.] \emph{Deliver a defensible STAR application.} Success requires
independent R replication plus school-cluster bootstrap, cyclic-order,
chronology, and sensitivity audits, with conclusions promoted only through a
pre-specified evidence hierarchy.
\end{description}

C1 and C4 are separated so the algorithm remains domain-neutral while the
application states its coding, selection, clustering, and interpretation
choices. \Cref{sec:background} defines the source and software gap;
\cref{sec:method,sec:results} evaluate O1--O4; and
\cref{sec:interpretation,sec:conclusions} distinguish supported conclusions
from exploratory patterns.

\section{Preliminaries and Related Work}
\label{sec:background}

\subsection{Graphs, assumptions, and PAGs}

Constraint-based discovery starts from statements of the form
\(X \perp Y \mid Z\) over observed variables \(V\), while allowing latent
variables \(L\) and selection variables \(S\) to be present. Under the
\emph{causal Markov condition}, graphical separation implies conditional
independence; under \emph{faithfulness}, observed independences arise from
graphical separation rather than exact cancellation. In a DAG, the relevant
separation criterion is d-separation:

\begin{definition}[d-separation]
A path \(p\) is blocked by a node set \(Z\) if and only if \(p\) contains a
chain or fork whose middle node is in \(Z\), or a collider whose middle node
is neither in \(Z\) nor an ancestor of \(Z\). If \(Z\) blocks every path
between \(X\) and \(Y\), they are d-separated given \(Z\)
\citep{pearl2009causality}.
\end{definition}

When causal sufficiency holds, these assumptions justify DAG-based methods
such as PC. Without causal sufficiency, the appropriate observed-variable
representation is a maximal ancestral graph (MAG)
\citep{richardson2002ancestral}. Marginalising over \(L\) and conditioning on
\(S\) can induce directed, bidirected, and undirected relations. Each missing
edge in a MAG corresponds to an \emph{m-separation}, which generalises
d-separation to mixed ancestral graphs and provides the criterion used by the
project's exact oracle. Because multiple MAGs may encode the same independence
model, the identifiable output is a PAG that records precisely the endpoint
features shared across a Markov equivalence class
\citep{zhang2008completeness}.

Every PAG adjacency has two endpoint marks. A circle leaves ancestry
unresolved; an arrowhead at \(Y\) rules out \(Y\) as an ancestor of \(X\); a
tail asserts ancestry in every represented MAG; a bidirected edge is
consistent with latent confounding but does not identify a particular latent
variable; and an undirected edge reflects selection structure. An adjacency
indicates only that the tested conditioning sets did not separate the pair.
Neither an adjacency nor an endpoint by itself identifies a structural
coefficient, an adjustment set, or an interventional contrast
\citep{pearl2009causality}. Because sample-based CI tests are susceptible to
error, recovery under an exact oracle evaluates graph logic independently of
finite-sample accuracy. Of the discovery families reviewed by
\citet{glymour2019review}, score-based searches such as GES and standard
functional causal models such as LiNGAM assume causal sufficiency. The
constraint-based FCI family is therefore the appropriate choice when latent
confounding and selection need to be represented.

\subsection{Standard FCI and its historical boundary}

FCI does not require every common cause of the measured variables to be
observed \citep[Ch.~6]{spirtes2000causation}. A PC-style search removes
\(X - Y\) when the pair is separated by some subset of their current
neighbours. In the presence of latent variables, however, a separable pair
may remain adjacent because a required separator can include a node that is
no longer adjacent to either endpoint. The book uses D-SEP to characterise
where an observed separator may lie and, because D-SEP cannot be computed
from a partial graph, introduces an observable superset:

\begin{definition}[Possible-D-SEP]
For an ordered pair \((A,B)\) in a partially oriented graph, a node
\(V \neq A\) belongs to \(\mathrm{Possible\text{-}D\text{-}SEP}(A,B)\) if
some path from \(A\) to \(V\) has every internal triple either a collider or
a triangle whose middle node is not a definite noncollider
\citep{spirtes2000causation}.
\end{definition}

This construction is intentionally permissive and must be formed
\emph{separately} for each ordered endpoint. Searching in only one direction
or combining the two pools changes the algorithmic contract. The complete
schedule consists of a PC-style adjacency search, initial orientation of
unshielded colliders, ordered Possible-D-SEP refinement, endpoint resetting
and collider reconstruction from the final separators, and orientation
closure. Given correct population-level CI decisions, Theorem 6.4 of the
source guarantees a correct \emph{partially oriented inducing path graph}.
The authors nevertheless state that they did not know whether the result was
maximally informative. Complete orientation therefore relies on the later
rules of \citet{zhang2008completeness} and is not retrospectively attributed
to the 2000 source.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{\FCIplus{}}{FCI+}: Algorithm 2}

\citet{claassen2013learning} ask whether causal discovery remains
intrinsically exponential when the observed structure is sparse. Their
central result, restated in the form used throughout this report:

\begin{theorem}[\citealp{claassen2013learning}]
Assume a constant-time exact independence oracle, a distribution faithful to
a causal DAG, and an observed-variable MAG of maximum degree at most a
constant \(k\). Then Algorithm~2 (\FCIplus{}) returns the completed PAG over
\(N\) observed variables using \(O(N^{2(k+2)})\) CI queries in the worst
case.
\end{theorem}

The scope of this result is narrower than the paper's title suggests: it
bounds oracle queries, but does not render a statistical test exact or
constant-time. A pair that is nonadjacent in the true MAG may survive a
bounded adjacency search if every useful separator contains a node outside
the union of the endpoints' adjacencies. Such a spurious adjacency is called
a \emph{D-sep link}. Instead of searching a large Possible-D-SEP pool,
\FCIplus{} encodes invariant non-ancestry in an \emph{augmented skeleton}
\(G^{+}\): when a known minimal independence
\(X \perp Y \mid S\) becomes dependence given \(S \cup \{A\}\) for a suitable
adjacent \(A\), an invariant arrowhead at \(A\) is supported. In \(G^{+}\), a
D-sep link \(X - Y\) appears as the middle edge of
\(U \leftrightarrow X \leftrightarrow Y \leftrightarrow V\) with \(U\),
\(V\) nonadjacent and cross-direction paths not running against an
arrowhead; the pattern only \emph{proposes} a candidate, and independence
evidence determines whether deletion is warranted. Conditioning sets are
drawn from the recursive hierarchy \(\mathrm{HIE}(X, I)\), defined as the
fixed point obtained by repeatedly adding the recorded minimal separators of
its members. Since candidates may depend on one another, each successful
removal rebuilds the augmented skeleton and candidate list
(\cref{fig:workflow}). The arXiv supplement's abstract states
\(N^{2(k+1)}\), but its theorem and proof agree with the UAI paper's
\(N^{2(k+2)}\). Algorithm~2 invokes complete FCI orientation, so its
completed-output claim depends on
\citet{zhang2008completeness}.

\subsection{What the sources do not establish}

Neither source guarantees recovery in finite samples, since both sets of
theoretical results assume correct CI decisions. Neither source identifies a
unique DAG, an adjustment set, or a treatment effect, and a bidirected
pattern neither names nor measures a hidden cause. The 2000 source establishes
neither maximal orientation nor a polynomial bound. The 2013 source, in turn,
establishes no finite-sample superiority and offers no guarantee when \(k\)
is misspecified: a user-supplied bound is not evidence that the assumption
holds. Its authors also note that hierarchy-derived conditioning sets can be
large, thereby reducing power in finite samples.

\subsection{Existing software}

\Cref{tab:capability} summarises the dated capability comparison: \code{pcalg}
\citep{kalisch2012pcalg} exposes both algorithms and accepts user-defined CI
tests, but its \code{fciPlus} result carries materially less separator
provenance; causal-learn \citep{causallearn2026} exposes standard FCI but no
public Claassen \FCIplus{} entry point; Tetrad \citep{tetrad2026} provides

\section{Methodology}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Package design}

The package translates the requirements set out in \cref{sec:background}
into inspectable components while keeping algorithmic implementation separate
from application policy. Reusable code resides in \code{src/fci\_engine},
the STAR workflow in \code{case\_studies/tennessee\_star}, and the estimator
contains no STAR-specific choices. The two algorithms have separate entry
points and explicit paper profiles, preventing a finite-sample default from
silently substituting for the source algorithm. \code{FCIConfig.paper()}
specifies unbounded depth, immediate
deletion, first-found separators, enabled Possible-D-SEP, nonconservative
colliders, and the historical \code{spirtes\_2000} closure;
\code{FCIPlusConfig.paper(k)} requires an explicit \(k\) for both
conditioning depth and sparsity bound, disables Possible-D-SEP, and selects
the complete \code{standard} closure required by Algorithm~2's output claim.
These profiles govern execution but do not validate theorem assumptions;
setting \(k=3\) does not establish the true MAG's degree.

The \code{PAG} class stores a mark at both ends of every adjacency, explicitly
representing tails, arrowheads, circles, and absence. All searches share the
\code{CITest.test(data, x, y, cond\_set)} interface, with built-in Fisher-Z,
missing-value Fisher-Z, chi-square, G-square, and kernel tests. Cache
canonicalisation distinguishes logical CI requests from cache hits and
executed statistics, as required when counting oracle queries.

\code{FCI.fit} makes the source-prescribed stage order visible and labels
Possible-D-SEP removals with the source \code{pdsep}.
\code{FCIPlus.fit} intentionally avoids calling Possible-D-SEP, passing the
initial skeleton instead to the Algorithm~2 sequence: augmented-skeleton
construction, bidirected witness patterns, paired endpoint bases, hierarchy
fixed points, separator testing and minimisation, graph updating, and
candidate rebuilding. Its diagnostics count candidates, revisits, hierarchy
and D-SEP queries, and the largest conditioning set; these are accounting
measures, not a proof of the bound. A single dispatcher provides
five orientation schedules: \code{spirtes\_2000}, the historical closure
used by the standard-FCI paper profile; \code{standard}, the modern complete
schedule required by the \FCIplus{} paper profile; and \code{conservative},
\code{leaf}, and \code{robust}, finite-sample engineering policies
supporting no completed-PAG claim. A regression verifies that
\code{spirtes\_2000} stops before a selection-bias tail completion that
\code{standard} orients, making the historical boundary executable.
\code{FCIResult} binds the final graph to the evidence that produced it ---
configuration, separators with stage sources, logical CI counts, and named
CI and orientation traces; \code{explain\_edge} returns every decision
behind a pair, so a plotted arrow cannot detach from the profile, test,
threshold, and rule that created it.

The project comprises a 9,559-line, 38-module algorithm package, 367 tests,
a 3,052-line STAR application, an independent 396-line R runner, and
supporting scripts. It includes \code{py.typed} and passes Ruff, strict MyPy,
wheel building, and Pytest on Python 3.9--3.13. These checks establish
interface and packaging consistency, not source faithfulness, sparsity, or
empirical conclusions.

The practical profile exchanges additional computation for greater stability.
Stable deletion reduces order dependence; max-\(p\) separators use more CI
queries to seek stronger independence evidence; conservative collider and
tail policies retain circles; and automatic \(\alpha\) follows a documented
sample-size heuristic. Consequently, ``paper \FCIplus{}'' is evaluated for
source fidelity and query efficiency, whereas ``robust \FCIplus{}'' is the
preferred policy for the application because it satisfies the stated audit
criteria while producing less-specific endpoints. This is a preference for
the present application, not a universal ranking.

\subsection{Validation and comparison design}

The most stringent tests replace sample-based CI decisions with exact
m-separation in a known MAG, thereby separating search correctness from
sampling error. Three independently specified targets are based on published
structures: the
canonical Figure~4(b) D-SEP example of \citet{claassen2013learning}, a
latent-variable reference graph from the FCI literature, and an
orientation-rule reference graph associated with
\citet{zhang2008completeness}. Finite-sample behaviour is subsequently
evaluated on 15 deterministic latent-SEM cases: five graph families, three
seeds per family, 2,500 observations, and known oracle PAGs.

The executed software comparison evaluates identical committed data under
recorded settings. The benchmark reuses the 15 known-truth runs, every
method receiving the same numeric matrix and variable order. Fixed paper and
reference rows use Fisher-Z at \(\alpha = 0.001\); robust \FCIplus{} uses
its documented sample-size rule, which resolves to \(\alpha = 0.01\) here
--- intentional: that row evaluates the public robust policy, not a literal
Algorithm~2 reproduction. Exact F1 requires both endpoint marks to match;
semantic F1 additionally credits endpoint patterns compatible with the
target ancestral constraints; endpoint accuracy scores individual marks. The
common conversion represents each edge as an unordered pair with two semantic
marks. Where interfaces permit, data, order, alpha, and CI semantics are
fixed; remaining interface mismatches are disclosed.

\subsection{STAR data and panels}

The Tennessee Student/Teacher Achievement Ratio project was a large
randomized experiment spanning kindergarten to grade 3. It assigned students
and teachers to small classes (approximately 13--17 students), regular
classes (approximately 22--25), or regular classes supported by a full-time
aide
\citep{word1990star}; it remains a central source of evidence on class size
\citep{krueger1999experimental}. This analysis uses the official Harvard
Dataverse release \citep{achilles2008star}: 11,601 student records, 379
variables, CC0 licence, codes checked against the 146-page user guide.

Because randomization provides a stronger basis for estimating the class-size
effect than structure learning, FCI is not used to rediscover that effect.
Instead, the analysis addresses structural questions: the attrition pattern
in grade-3 observation, persistence of the early--later achievement
adjacency, the consequences of including an early post-assignment outcome,
agreement across implementations, and the search savings of \FCIplus{}. The
experimental design also provides an external audit. If a data-only PAG
implies an implausible direction into a pre-treatment variable or latent
confounding of randomized assignment, the result is treated as a discrepancy
rather than interpreted literally.

The analysis includes 6,325 students with a kindergarten class assignment
across 79 schools. They are organised into three panels that are deliberately
kept separate because each represents a different statistical question
(\cref{tab:panels}). Every model input is an integer-coded discrete category:
class arm, sex, ethnicity (with small source categories combined), free-lunch
status, school context, entry-age quantiles, binned teacher experience, and
reading-plus-mathematics scaled scores transformed into achievement
quartiles. The exact numerical tables supplied to the algorithms are
committed to the repository.

\subsection{Discovery protocol and audits}

Before structure discovery, randomized-arm contrasts are calculated for
kindergarten scores, grade-3 scores among students for whom both scores are
available, and the probability that both scores are observed. Uncertainty is
estimated from 1,000 kindergarten-school bootstrap samples, and these
design-based contrasts are reported separately from the discovery results.

Discovery uses the likelihood-ratio G-square test,
\(G^2 = 2 \sum_s \sum_{x,y} O_{xys} \log ( O_{xys} / E_{xys} )\), at
\(\alpha = 0.05\) --- chosen because every panel variable is an
integer-coded category, where Fisher-Z's Gaussian-linear assumptions do not
apply, at the cost of sparse contingency cells under high-order
conditioning. Standard FCI runs its unbounded paper schedule; paper
\FCIplus{} uses \(k = 3\); robust \FCIplus{} uses the practical profile
(stable skeleton, strongest separating sets, conservative colliders, robust
orientation, depth and sparsity bound 3); the independent reference is
\code{pcalg::fciPlus} with selection bias enabled and no exposed \(k\). No
temporal priors are supplied to discovery; reversals are audited after
fitting.

Alternative choices were excluded for specific reasons. PC and GES require
causal sufficiency; Fisher-Z is poorly matched to the discrete panels; kernel
CI is substantially more expensive and does not address clustered sampling;
and temporal priors would mask chronology failures rather than reveal them.
A formal model of the randomized treatment effect complements the structural
question and is therefore kept separate from the discovery analysis.

Stability is examined through three audits. First, 100 school-cluster
bootstrap refits are performed for each algorithm and panel, recording how
often each unordered adjacency appears; this frequency is an exploratory
diagnostic, not a posterior probability. Second, a cyclic-order audit refits
all profiles and the R reference under every cyclic shift of the variable
list. Third, the focused panel is evaluated over a sensitivity grid with
\(\alpha \in \{0.01, 0.05\}\), three or four quantile bins, and
\(k \in \{2,3,4\}\).

A conclusion is promoted at the skeleton level only if the adjacency survives
every cyclic shift and has a high bootstrap frequency. A directional claim
must additionally have invariant endpoint marks, agree with measurement time
and randomization, and show no material reversal in sensitivity analyses. If
these conditions fail, direction is reported as unresolved rather than the
edge being discarded.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Exact-oracle validation}

Both algorithms recover each of the three encoded targets. Figure~4(b) is
also evaluated under four variable orders. Its MAG contains six edges
(\(Z \rightarrow U\), \(Z \rightarrow V\), \(U \leftrightarrow X\),
\(V \rightarrow X\), \(U \rightarrow Y\), \(V \leftrightarrow Y\)); the
adjacency stage retains a false \(X - Y\) link although
\(X \perp Y \mid \{U,V,Z\}\), with \(Z\) adjacent to neither endpoint. The
source presents this separator but does not provide an endpoint-complete PAG;
the completed comparison target is therefore derived in the repository
(\cref{fig:figure4}). In this example, paper-aligned standard FCI requires
102 CI queries and \FCIplus{} requires 63, a reduction of 38.2\%, while both
recover the exact target. The values, target hash, and separator provenance
are frozen in
\code{reports/data/\brk oracle\_validation\_summary.csv}.

\subsection{Finite-sample known-truth benchmark}

On a seeded latent linear SEM over the same reference graph, at
\(N = 5{,}000\) \FCIplus{} returns an extra \(X \leftrightarrow Y\)
(exact-edge F1 0.923) while standard FCI recovers the exact PAG; at
\(N = 50{,}000\) both are exact --- a reproducible fixture, not a universal
threshold.

Across the 15 cases (the first three rows of
\cref{tab:executed-software-benchmark}), the robust profile achieves higher
mean skeleton and endpoint scores at approximately 2.04 times the CI cost of
paper \FCIplus{}. This is not universal superiority, but shows that the STAR
stability gain is not obtained simply by discarding structure. Fewer CI tests
need not improve accuracy because the algorithms follow different paths after
the initial skeleton. Finite-sample results remain sensitive to CI error and
tuning despite exact-oracle validation.

\subsection{Structure discovery across the three panels}

For the attrition panel, the three implementations show substantial agreement
at the skeleton level (\cref{fig:pag-attrition}; costs and agreement are
reported in \cref{tab:agreement-performance}). None retains a direct
\code{K\_Class}--\code{Grade3\_Observed} adjacency, which appears in only
12--16\% of school bootstraps. By contrast,
\code{K\_Achievement}--\code{Grade3\_Observed} is adjacent in 100\% of
bootstrap fits for all three local profiles, and the free-lunch/observation
adjacency is likewise stable. Thus, later data availability is not
independent of early achievement and measured context. The endpoint
interpretations nevertheless diverge. Standard FCI produces the impossible
\emph{grade-3 observation \(\rightarrow\) kindergarten achievement};
self-\FCIplus{} retains a circle; and R-\FCIplus{} produces the temporally
admissible direction. Although the R result is more plausible, software
identity cannot establish correctness. Nor does the PAG prove a missingness
mechanism, which requires an explicit estimand and additional assumptions.

Excluding kindergarten achievement changes the result. All three algorithms
return the same 10-edge PAG, rather than merely the same skeleton, including

focused panel conditions on observed grade-3 outcomes and omits early
post-assignment achievement. Selection on observation, school-level

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:interpretation}

\subsection{Evidence hierarchy}

The evidence hierarchy prevents a definite PAG mark from outweighing the
experimental design. Tier 1 is randomized assignment with a school-cluster
contrast. Tier 2 comprises adjacencies replicated across methods, resamples,
orders, and sensitivity settings, supporting skeleton-level claims but not
magnitude or direction. Tier 3 contains stable skeletons with circles or
endpoint disagreement. Tier 4 includes implementation-, order-, or
chronology-dependent endpoints and is diagnostic only. Tier 5 contains
threshold-sensitive or complete-case patterns requiring external validation.

\subsection{What can and cannot be concluded}

The Tier-1 result, a 13.90-point small-class contrast, is consistent with an
early benefit. It is not, however, an identified causal effect within the
post-assignment observed subset without further assumptions about missing
outcomes, compliance, and the original randomization blocks. Aide contrasts
are close to zero at both time points, providing no evidence that an aide
reproduces the small-class advantage. The achievement--achievement and
achievement--observation adjacencies recur across all fits and bootstraps,
supporting a robust \emph{skeleton-level} relationship among early
performance, later performance, and later availability (Tier 2). The
full-data attrition PAGs contain no direct class-arm/observation adjacency,
and its bootstrap frequency is low. Concern about selective attrition is
therefore supported without evidence of a stable direct arm effect on
observation.

Several conclusions remain unsupported. No unique DAG is identified because
each PAG represents an equivalence class, and FCI supplies no effect
magnitude. A bidirected edge does not prove the existence of a hidden
confounder; in the focused panel, such an interpretation conflicts with known
randomization unless selection or post-treatment conditioning is taken into
account. Temporal reversals are diagnostics of the algorithm rather than
credible chronology. Agreement across implementations is also not proof,
since shared CI decisions and rules can reproduce the same incorrect
endpoint. The 26 recorded shifts constitute a finite audit rather than a
proof of order invariance, and bootstrap frequency is not a posterior
probability. Finally, absence is conditional on policy: failure to retain an
edge under one node set, test, and threshold does not demonstrate a zero
effect.

\subsection{Limitations of the method}

Guarantees for the FCI family depend on faithfulness and correct CI decisions,
as illustrated in practice by the threshold-sensitive edge in the focused
panel. Because the true degree of the STAR MAG is unobserved, \(k=3\) is an
analysis choice rather than a verified sparsity condition. Discretisation
makes G-square testing possible, but loses within-bin information and can
produce sparse conditional tables. The row-wise CI test is not robust to
clustering. School-level bootstrapping reveals sensitivity to school
composition, but does not correct this mismatch. Finally, version 0.1.0 and
the manually specified oracle targets provide strong regression evidence,
not a formal proof of correctness or an exhaustive check of MAG equivalence.

A deterministic scaling audit, covering 5 to 80 nodes in both a directed
chain and the Figure~4(b) core with additional isolated variables, recovers
the exact target skeleton in every run. It also reveals that the query
advantage is not universal. \FCIplus{} performs better on the five-node core
(62 versus 101 queries), but uses 5,162 queries versus 3,251 at 80 nodes and
24,338 versus 12,638 on the 80-node chain. This is consistent with a
worst-case bound: augmentation carries a genuine cost when targeted D-SEP
search offers little benefit.

\subsection{Limitations of the STAR study}

Both the longitudinal and focused panels condition on observed grade-3 scores,
which may open selection paths. Kindergarten achievement is measured after
assignment: including it may block part of the class-size pathway, whereas
excluding it may leave post-treatment dependence. Presenting both panels
makes this modelling choice visible but does not establish either model as
correct. The analysis does not reconstruct the original randomization blocks,
class switching, compliance, or school-specific procedures. Moreover, the
composite score leaves subject-specific and subgroup effects unexplored.

\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusions}

All four objectives were achieved, subject to clearly stated limits.
\textbf{O1 was met}: the package provides source-located profiles of FCI and
Algorithm~2 with typed, auditable outputs. \textbf{O2 was met}: it recovers
all three exact targets, passes 367 tests, and quantifies finite-sample error
through the 15-run benchmark. \textbf{O3 was met within the available
interfaces}: matched causal-learn and \code{pcalg} runs reproduce the
aggregate paper-profile scores, although neither exposes every control or
diagnostic. \textbf{O4 was met within its structural scope}: the STAR
workflow completes all planned replication and stability audits, while
identifying neither a formal intention-to-treat effect nor a unique causal
graph.

The STAR evidence supports a 13.90-point observed kindergarten advantage for
small classes, no comparable benefit from an aide, a stable early--later
achievement structure, and selective later observation. Nevertheless, the
independently replicated 10-edge focused PAG remains threshold-sensitive, and
some endpoints vary with ordering or conflict with chronology. By retaining
circles, robust \FCIplus{} avoids these literal reversals at 1.64--1.85 times
the CI cost of the paper profile. This improves reliability without improving
identifiability.

The main engineering challenge was translating ordered Possible-D-SEP pools
and Algorithm~2's hierarchy and revisit semantics into executable invariants.
Oracle-first validation, separation of algorithm from application, and
committed seeds and artifacts proved most effective. Literal paper profiles
produced unstable endpoints on real data, so robust \FCIplus{} is preferred
for this application; paper \FCIplus{} remains the fidelity and
query-efficiency reference.

The central contribution is an auditable account of conditional-independence
constraints and modelling sensitivity, rather than a rediscovered causal
model of STAR. Future work should reconstruct the randomization strata for a
formal intention-to-treat analysis; introduce cluster-aware CI tests and
endpoint-level stability summaries; analyse reading and mathematics
separately; evaluate temporal background knowledge in a distinct analysis;
and derive target PAGs using an exhaustive MAG-equivalence oracle.
